## The book

`{77}` "a little room, where sat two little children, each one in his chair. The name of the eldest was Passion, and the name of the other Patience. Passion seemed to be much discontented; but Patience was very quiet." Passion "will have all now"; Patience "is willing to wait". "Then I saw that one came to Passion, and brought him a bag of treasure, and poured it down at his feet, the which he took up and rejoiced therein, and withal laughed Patience to scorn. But I beheld but a while, and he had lavished all away, and had nothing left him but rags." `{78}`, `{79}`: so are the men of this world; the things which are seen are temporal, the things not seen eternal (Bunyan's margin: 2 Cor. 4:18; Luke 16:25).

## How it plays now (build 650; the room has not changed since build 507)

1. N: "In a little room sat two little children. The name of the eldest was Passion, and of the other, Patience."
2. C: "Why is Passion so discontented?"; I: "He must wait for his best things till next year, but he will have all now. Patience is willing to wait."; N: "Then one came to Passion, and brought him a bag of treasure."; TIP "Touch the bag." (act `bag`).
3. The purse tips, the gold pours at his feet with chinks and glints, lies a while, then each coin rises as a soap bubble (after Chardin), one great bubble last; Passion in rags dissolves in over the fine boy.
4. I: "So are the men of this world: they must have all their good things now."; C: "Then it is best to wait for things to come."; I: "You say the truth. For the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal." Then the passage on to the fire room.
