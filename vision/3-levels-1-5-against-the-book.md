# Book audit: Levels 1 to 5 against Bunyan (Part One, {10} to {94})

Read: the book file {10} to {95} in full; every spoken line in `index.html` (INTRO ... END, LEVEL2_OPENING), `slough.js`, `hill.js`, `rooms.js`, `cross.js`; CLAUDE.md's book rules, level sections and departure lists. Nothing in the repo was changed. Staging is taken from code comments and CLAUDE.md; I did not play the game or look at stills.

## The short verdict

The game is faithful in ORDER and in WORDS: nearly every line is a real Bunyan sentence made plain, said by the right person, and there are no serious order faults. Where it falls short is in the book's ARGUMENT. Two rounds of cutting ("less waiting") removed mostly the sentences that say WHY: why he flees, why he cannot take the burden off himself, why Legality cannot help, what the Slough is, and the promise that the burden will fall "of itself". What is left is a beautiful sequence of events whose meaning a child of 7 to 10 could miss. The burden's fall at the Cross is staged exactly right (it falls by itself), but the three earlier sentences that set it up are all currently absent from the game.

The five things that matter most, in order:

1. **"I cannot get it off myself" is never said** ({39}), and **"it will fall from your back of itself"** ({68}) is not in the game at all, because the Good-will scene is not built. The Cross has no set-up.
2. **Why he runs is never said.** The city's coming destruction ({11}, {19}) and what "the wrath to come" is are both absent; and he never tells his family, so on the "Run on" path a father runs from his crying children with his fingers in his ears and no reason given.
3. **Why Legality cannot free him is never said** ({57}). "Morality" and "Legality" are only names to a child; Level 3 reads as "he took a wrong turn near a scary mountain", not "trying to be good enough cannot take the burden off".
4. **At the Cross nobody says whose cross it is or that the sight of it did this** ({94}: "very surprising to him, that the sight of the cross should thus ease him of his burden"; the song's "blest rather be the Man that there was put to shame for me").
5. **The whole Good-will scene is a hole in the story** ({60} to {69}): Level 3 ends as the door opens and Level 4 begins at the Interpreter's door with "the man at the gate said ...", a talk the child never saw.

Each can be mended with one short line or by rewording an existing line (list in section F). Level 5's lines are not yet recorded, so changes there are free.

## Level 1, City of Destruction ({10} to {24})

### (a) Where it falls short, most important first

1. **The danger to the city** ({11}: "this our city will be burned with fire from heaven"; {19}: "you dwell ... in the City of Destruction ... dying there, sooner or later, you will sink lower than the grave"). The game's only statement is Christian's "this book tells me I am in great danger". The danger is his alone, and never named. The city's name appears only on the contents line. So "Flee from the wrath to come" has nothing behind it: a child cannot tell what he flees or why the neighbours should come too.
2. **He told his family and they would not believe him** ({11}). Listed as left out, but it carries the moral weight of the level's hardest picture. Without it he seems to leave them without a word. The game's own line "I must find the way, for all of us" (ours) is only heard on the "Stop and look back" path; on the book's path ("Run on") there is nothing.
3. **What the burden is.** The book lets the margin say it (Ps. 38:4, "mine iniquities ... as an heavy burden") and {15} ("this burden ... will sink me lower than the grave"). The game keeps "this burden on my back will sink me", which is good, but nothing in Levels 1 to 3 ever calls it sin. The first time a child hears the word is in the Interpreter's parlour ("The dust is his sin"), about a different picture. (See section E.)
4. **"What shall I do to be saved?"** ({12}). The game has only the first cry, "What shall I do?" ({10}). The second cry in the fields, with its three added words, is the question the whole book answers. FIELDS is a storyteller line ("He walked alone in the fields, reading his book and praying") that could carry it.
5. **What the book in his hand teaches.** Kept: he reads, weeps, "this book tells me". Dropped: Christian pointing others to it ({21} "Read it so, if you will, in my book"; {23} "read here in this book"), which is why Obstinate says "Away with your book". Obstinate's line survives but now answers nothing.
6. **What he seeks** ({21}: "an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, and that fadeth not away"). ONWARD keeps "All you leave behind is not worth a little of what I seek", which is the right sentence. Fine as it is.
7. **Pliable's motive** ({22}, {23}): kept well ("If what Christian says is true, the things he seeks are better than ours"). His question "do you know the way?" and Christian's "I am directed by a man whose name is Evangelist, to a little gate" ({24}) are cut; small loss.
8. **The dreamer's frame**: present and good. The contents page carries {10}'s first sentence; INTRO opens "I dreamed a dream"; RUNS keeps "So I saw in my dream". (Level 5 keeps "I saw it no more".) Levels 2 to 4 have no "I saw in my dream"; not needed.
9. **Evangelist's roll and "the wrath to come"**: present, in Bunyan's words, with the keepsake close-up and his own margin (Matt. iii. 7). Good. Only the meaning of "wrath to come" is missing (item 1).

### (b) Departures not in CLAUDE.md's list

- **Christian's answer to Evangelist is softened**: book {14} "I am condemned to die, and after that to come to judgement"; game "I am in great danger". Reasonable for the age, but it is a departure and is not listed.
- **The roll is given by the storyteller's sentence and read aloud by him**; in the book Christian reads it ({17}). Trivial.
- **"Two of them ... set out to bring him back by force"** is said BEFORE they overtake him, as in the book, but each stands first in his own doorway (listed). Fine.
- **The city gate at the end may be taken for the wicket-gate.** Evangelist has just said "you will see the gate. Knock"; minutes later the child pushes open "a great gate". The departure list has the gate as ours, but not this confusion. A child may well think Level 1 ends with the gate Evangelist meant. (Fix: "the city gate" in GATEWORDS; one word.)
- **Amazing Grace** (Newton, 1772) is a century later than the book (1678). A design choice the owner loves; simply not listed as a departure.
- **Listed departure that hurts a little**: HOME's "No. I cannot stay here. My burden is too heavy." gives the burden as the reason to leave; the book's reason is the city's doom and the life ahead. Tolerable.

### (d) Order

Sound. One note: in the book he is already in the fields when the story's action begins and runs past "his own door" on the way; the game's "out, then back" agrees with that. The neighbours' "why have you come?" exchange is called out before they overtake him (book: after); harmless.

### (e) Meaning risks

- The father-leaves-family picture without {11} (above). This is the one most likely to trouble a child or a parent watching.
- The burden is never tied to sin before Level 4; a child can reasonably take it for sadness or worry.
- "Stop and look back" and "Go home" teach nothing false (nothing is lost, and he goes on by himself), and the "for all of us" line is a kind gloss consistent with {11} ("to pray for and pity them").

## Level 2, Slough of Despond ({25} to {36})

### (a) Where it falls short

1. **What the Slough is.** One storyteller line survives: "Many fears and doubts came over him, and settled in that place" ({33}), with the dark and the drums. That is the right sentence and the right picture. What is lost is the name's meaning: "Despond" is not a word a 7-year-old knows, and FALL now says only "Its name was Despond."
2. **The steps.** Help asks "Why did you not look for the steps?" and Christian answers "Fear followed me so hard that I fell in." Then the lights find the stones. Since build 391 nothing says the steps were always there ({35}: "certain good and substantial steps, placed even through the very midst"). The picture can carry it, but only if the stones are plainly seen to have been there before he fell (they are "hidden until its light finds it"), so the lesson may read as "the stones appear afterwards" instead of "they were there and fear hid them".
3. **Pliable's motive** ({26} to {28}, {30}). After two cuts his whole part is: "Tell me more of the place where we are going", "Ah, neighbor Christian, where are you now?", "You can have your fine country all to yourself!" The cut line "I'm glad to hear it! Come on, let's walk faster!" was the one that showed him eager for the reward and then quitting at the first trouble; and Bunyan's best line for him, "Is this the happiness you have told me all this while of?", is gone. He still reads as a fair-weather friend from the picture (he climbs out on his own side), so this is a moderate loss.
4. **"He will bestow it upon us freely"** ({28}: "If we be truly willing to have it, he will bestow it upon us freely"). Pliable asks how they shall share in it, and this is Christian's answer: the first statement of grace in the book. Never in the game.
5. **Christian reads the promises from his book** ({27}: "I will read of them in my book ... it was made by Him that cannot lie"). In the game he has no book after Level 1 (build 189, for picture reasons).
6. **"Set him upon sound ground"** ({32}, Ps. 40:2 in the margin: "He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock"). Shown, not said. Good candidate for a quiet margin reference rather than words.
7. {34} (the King's labourers, the cart-loads of instructions) and {36} (Pliable sneaking among his neighbours): rightly left out.

### (b) Departures not listed

- **Christian has no book from Level 2 on**, though the book has him read from it to Pliable ({27}) and still holding it when he meets Wiseman ({41}: "By reading this book in my hand"). CLAUDE.md records the decision (build 189) but it is not in the departures list.
- **Christian's opening answer joins two of his answers and drops their giver**: {27} "He that is owner of the place will wipe all tears from our eyes". The list notes the swapped order, not the dropped "owner of the place".
- **Help's first question and the "wrath to come" answer are cut** ({31}): listed. Effect: Help's FIRST words to a man drowning in mud are now a reproach ("Why did you not look for the steps?"). In the book he first asks what he is doing there. It reads a little hard.
- Listed and fine: the choice, the sinking deeper, Help walking away, the city in the sky, the dark and drums.

### (c) Lines

|---|---|---|

| N: "They fell into the bog. Its name was Despond." | {29} "The name of the slough was Despond." | "They fell into a bog. Its name was the Slough of Despond." (the famous name whole; the fears line then explains it) |

The Lord of that place will wipe away all tears, and He gives it freely." (if one line can be afforded here, this is the one; otherwise leave) |

### (d) Order

Sound. FEARS is given to the storyteller mid-wade (listed). The choice now waits until Pliable is gone, as in the book.

### (e) Meaning risks

- **This level is the game's best picture of grace before the Cross**: the child holds to wade, the holding does less and less, he "could not get out, because of the burden", and only Help's hand lifts him. The child's act is to TAKE the hand. That is exactly right and should be protected in any later rework.
- "Go back, as Pliable did" costs nothing; fine for the age.
- The Slough as fear and doubt: said once, shown strongly. Enough, if "Despond" is given its full name.

## Level 3, Mr. Worldly Wiseman and the hill ({37} to {60})

### (a) Where it falls short

1. **"Get it off myself, I cannot"** ({39}: "get it off myself, I cannot; nor is there any man in our country that can take it off my shoulders"). Cut with the rest of the talk. This is the plainest statement in the first sixty sections that he cannot save himself, and it is what makes Wiseman's offer a temptation.
2. **Why Legality cannot help** ({57}: "No man was as yet ever rid of his burden by him; no, nor ever is like to be ... by the deeds of the law no man living can be rid of his burden"). The game: "That man was Worldly Wiseman. Mr. Legality cannot free you." True, but with no reason, and "Morality" and "Legality" mean nothing to the audience. The level's point (being good, keeping rules, cannot lift the burden) is not available to a child.
3. **What the hill is** ({57}: "this Mount Sinai"; the words out of the mountain, Gal. 3:10). The hill is the Law. The game makes it a magnificent volcano. Nothing connects it to the "Law" that the Interpreter names in the parlour one level later ("He who swept first is the Law"). A single word would tie the two together.
4. **"His burden now seemed heavier to him than while he was in his way"** ({46}). Cut as words in build 164; the burden "groans" once at Wiseman's line. This is a show-don't-tell gift: he should visibly bow lower and slow under the hill (the code already makes holding do "less and less").
5. **Wiseman's real lure**: an easy life ({42} "safety, friendship, and content"; {43} houses, honest neighbours "in credit and good fashion") and, in Evangelist's words, a way that "saveth him best from the cross" ({53}). The game keeps "There is no more dangerous way in all the world!" and the sweet tune and warm village windows, which carry it well in picture and sound.
6. **Who sent him** ({39} "his name, as I remember, is Evangelist"; {40} "I beshrew him for his counsel!"). With "Who sent you this way?" cut, Wiseman's "There is no more dangerous way" no longer attacks Evangelist's counsel; and Evangelist's return loses its sting ("Did not I direct thee the way?").
7. **Evangelist's warning on parting** ({59}: "take heed that thou turn not aside again"). Cut. Small.
8. **"Knock, and it shall be opened unto you"** ({59}, Matt. 7:8) is on the owner's own list of famous phrases to keep, but since build 366 it is not spoken: it is cut into the stone over the gate "too small to read in the scene" and seen only if the child touches a glinting stone. A child can finish Level 3 without ever meeting it.
9. **His knocking verse** ({60}): "May I come in? Will he open to me, sorry as I am?" keeps its heart ("sorry me ... an undeserving rebel"). Good.

### (b) Departures not listed

- **He falls at Evangelist's feet without being rebuked.** In the book he falls after Evangelist's two Hebrews verses and "Thou art the man that art running into this misery" ({51}, {52}). In the game he explains himself and at once cries "Woe is me". The list notes the cut verses but not that the fall has lost its cause.
- **Evangelist names Wiseman and Legality AFTER the forgiveness and after raising him** (book the same: {53} follows {52}); fine. But "Your sin is very great" then comes from a gentle Evangelist with no earlier severity (listed: "severe and dreadful countenance not shown"), and the child has not been told what the sin was. In the book: "thou hast forsaken the way that is good, to tread in forbidden paths".
- **The departures paragraph is out of date in places** (it still says he "falls at his feet (the weeping video)" in "How it goes", and that the storyteller names Wiseman). Housekeeping only.
- **Easter eggs on Sinai** (build 367 and the wide shot's "Touch the cloud, or the fire in the rock"): the child calls down lightning and makes the cracks flare for fun, lizards scurry, stones miss him. None contradicts the book, but they turn the place of the Law's terror into a toy while Christian "sweat and did quake for fear". Not listed as a departure in tone. The stones and the trembling serve the story; the touch-for-lightning does not.
- **The village stands beyond/behind the hill**; the book has the hill "by" the way to the house ({45}). Fine.
- Listed and worth re-reading: evening at the gate; three knocks; the child taking Evangelist's hand (good: it echoes Help's hand and is again "receive", not "do").

### (c) Lines

| Game | Book | Suggested |
|---|---|---|
| C: "To that little gate ahead, to be rid of my burden." | {38} "I am going to yonder wicket-gate ... to be rid of my heavy burden"; {39} "get it off myself, I cannot" | "To that little gate ahead. I cannot get this burden off myself." |
| E: "That man was Worldly Wiseman. Mr. Legality cannot free you." | {57} "This Legality ... is not able to set thee free from thy burden. No man was as yet ever rid of his burden by him" | "That man was Worldly Wiseman. No man was ever rid of his burden by Mr. Legality." (Bunyan's own sentence; a child hears "it has never worked") |
| N: "The hill hung so far over the way that Christian was afraid it would fall on his head." | {46}; {57} "this Mount Sinai" | "The hill was Mount Sinai. It hung so far over the way that Christian was afraid it would fall on his head." (optional; or give the name to Evangelist) |
| E: "Your sin is very great. Yet the man at the gate will receive you, for he has goodwill for men." | {59} "Thy sin is very great ... thou hast forsaken the way that is good ... yet will the man at the gate receive thee, for he has goodwill for men" | "You left the good way, and that was a great sin. Yet the man at the gate will receive you, for he has goodwill for men." |

### (d) Order

Sound against the book: Wiseman, the turn, the hill, Evangelist, the fall, the hand, the naming of Wiseman, "is there hope?", God-speed, haste, the gate, the knock, Good-will. The fire from the mountain comes before Evangelist as well as (in the book) after; the game keeps the first and has the fire SINK at "the man at the gate will receive you" (ours, listed), the reverse of {57} where fire and words come out to confirm Evangelist. It reads well, but it is the opposite of the book's beat.

### (e) Meaning risks

- **The biggest risk in Levels 1 to 3**: as cut, the level's lesson is "do not leave the path / do not listen to strangers". Bunyan's lesson is "you cannot be freed by being respectable and keeping the Law". Two reworded lines (section c) restore it at no cost in time.
- The mercy picture (Evangelist's hand, the warm light under the black hill) is very good and in the book's spirit ({52}).
- A child steers Christian off the way because the game requires the touch on the hill; there is no choice. That is right (the book gives him none) and the game does not punish.

## Level 4, the Wicket-Gate (planned) and the Interpreter's House ({60} to {92})

### (a) Where it falls short

1. **The Good-will scene does not exist in the game** ({60} to {69}). Missing as played today: "Here is a poor burdened sinner" ({61}); "I am willing with all my heart"; the pull and Beelzebub's arrows ({62}); "An open door is set before thee, and no man can shut it" ({63}); the narrow way, "as straight as a rule can make it" ({66}); and above all **{68}: "be content to bear it, until thou comest to the place of deliverance; for there it will fall from thy back of itself."** The planned words in CLAUDE.md are faithful and include {68}. Until it is built, the Cross arrives unannounced.
2. **In the plan itself, the grace sentence of the gate is cut with the recap**: {66} "We make no objections against any, notwithstanding all that they have done before they came hither. They are in no wise cast out" (John 6:37). It answers Level 3's "Will he open to me, sorry as I am?". The recap ({63} to {65}) is rightly cut; this one sentence should be kept back from it.
3. **The picture (room 1): who he is and what he does.** Kept: eyes to heaven, the best of books, the world behind, the crown, "the only man the Lord has sent to be your guide". Dropped: "the law of truth was written upon his lips", "it stood as if it pleaded with men", and the meaning "his work is to know and unfold dark things to sinners" ({73}). And, as CLAUDE.md admits, the repainted figure now looks out at us while the Interpreter says "His eyes are lifted up to heaven". The words and the picture disagree.
4. **The parlour (room 2)**: the best-kept room. Law stirs the dust, Gospel lays it; the child does both. "Law" and "Gospel" are hard words but they are Bunyan's two words and the acts explain them. If Level 3 names the hill as the Law (above), this room pays it off.
5. **Passion and Patience (room 3)**: well kept. Dropped: "The Governor of them would have him stay for his best things" (who makes him wait), Passion "laughed Patience to scorn" (painted boys cannot), and the Interpreter's "the glory of the next world will never wear out; but these are suddenly gone" ({79}), which is what the bubbles show.
6. **The fire (room 4)**: kept whole, and it names Christ and grace. This is the house's clearest statement that the work is kept going by another, "secretly". Good.
7. **The palace (room 5)**: Bunyan gives no explanation ("I think verily I know the meaning of this") and the game rightly gives none. Dropped: "So he went in, and was clothed with such garments as they" ({83}), which a picture can do (he appears in gold on the top). The margin (Acts 14:22, "through much tribulation") is the passage verse; a good use of Bunyan's own key.
8. **The cage and the dream (rooms 6, 7; off by default)**: faithfully and gently cut. The cage keeps the book's most pastoral exchange ("Is there no hope ...?" / "the Son of God is full of pity"). The dream keeps the trumpet, the cloud, "left behind ... not ready", and drops the pit; right for the age.
9. **Leaving**: "Yes, and they put me in hope and fear" is said even when the two fearful rooms were skipped, when nothing shown was fearful. Small.
10. **"Took him by the hand"**: the Interpreter leads Christian by the hand into every room ({74}, {77}, {80}, {82}, {84}, {89}). The passage has the Interpreter walking ahead with a candle. A pity, since "led by the hand" is the house's refrain and matches Help's and Evangelist's hands.

### (b) Departures not listed

- **The storyteller reads seven scripture verses in the passages** (build 315). The verses that are Bunyan's own margins (Rom. 5:20, 2 Cor. 12:9, Acts 14:22, Isa. 26:1) are a lovely way to surface them. Three are ours (Ps. 119:105, Matt. 6:20, Mark 13:37) and are said so in the build note but not in the departures list. Note Ps. 119:105 IS Bunyan's margin, only at {17} for "yonder shining light"; and for Passion and Patience his margin is Luke 16:25 or, better for children, the 2 Cor. 4:18 line the Interpreter already speaks. Also this is the storyteller saying words that are not the story (the owner's rule, build 108); the owner chose it, so only noting it.
- **Isaiah 26:1 is read just before the Interpreter's farewell and then {92}'s wall is described again by the storyteller**: the same image twice within a minute, and again in Level 5's first line.
- **The house plan** (rooms as garden, cellar, upstairs chamber, build 389): ours, drawn from the book's own words for each place; reasonable and unlisted.
- **Room 5: the child calls out the welcome by touching the people in gold** (in the book the voice comes of itself when he presses in): listed in a build note, not in the departure list.
- **Room 4: the order of the brave man's clip** puts the helmet on before the sword (noted in build 373).
- **The man at the Interpreter's door** ({70}, {71}: one comes, asks who is there, fetches the master) is skipped; trivial.
- **Level 3's end card goes straight to the Interpreter's door**: a temporary departure of sixty lines of the book, noted as "still to come".

### (d) Order

Rooms are in the book's order; the acts sit where the book's events sit. No faults. (Room 5: "Set down my name" before he arms, correct. Room 6: Christian asks "is there no hope" through the Interpreter, correct.)

### (e) Meaning risks

- **With the darker rooms off, the house ends on the palace**: the last thing a child sees before the Cross is a man who gets into the palace by drawing his sword and cutting his way through. Taken alone it says "the brave fight their way in". Bunyan balances it with the fire room before (grace kept by Christ) and the Cross after, and gives the name-writing first. The game keeps the name-writing as the child's act, which helps. A cheap further help: show him clothed in gold by those within ("was clothed with such garments as they"), which makes the end a gift.
- The cage for a child: "I cannot get out. Oh, now I cannot!" and "Oh, eternity, eternity!" are heavy even when chosen; the kept line "the Son of God is full of pity" is the right counterweight, and Christian's prayer closes it. Leave as it is, behind its switch.
- The parlour says plainly that the Law cannot clean and the Gospel does: the clearest doctrine in the game, in a child's act. Good.

## Level 5, the Cross ({92} to {94})

### (a) Where it falls short

1. **"The sight of the cross"** ({94}: "it was very surprising to him, that the sight of the cross should thus ease him of his burden"). LOOKED keeps "He stood still to look and wonder" but drops what he wondered at. This is the sentence that says the burden fell because of the cross and not because he had climbed the hill.
2. **Whose cross.** GLAD is exact Bunyan: "He hath given me rest by his sorrow, and life by his death." But "He" is never named in this level, the cross is empty (rightly, per the book), and Christ has been named only once, behind the wall in room 4. The cut song ends with the line that says it for a child: "Blest cross! blest sepulchre! blest rather be / The Man that there was put to shame for me!"
3. **The three gifts**: all four things are present and in the book's order: "Peace be unto thee"; "Thy sins be forgiven thee"; rags for new clothes; the mark on the forehead and the sealed roll, to look on as he runs and give in at the Celestial Gate. Shortfalls are in the pictures, and CLAUDE.md knows them: the new clothes are only a change of tone, and the roll in the tray still opens as Evangelist's unsealed one. For a child the coat is the most visible sign that "he's another man" ({94}'s verse: "Old things are past away, all's become new"); a tone shift will not be noticed.
4. **The mark in his forehead** (Eph. 1:13 margin: "sealed with that holy Spirit of promise"): present as a small light. Nothing more is needed; the book does not explain it here either.
5. **"I saw it no more"**: kept, in the dreamer's voice. Good.
6. **He ran** ({92}: "did burdened Christian run, but not without great difficulty"). OPEN says "Christian ran"; the child holds to WALK. Words and picture disagree slightly.

### (b) Departures not listed

- **He walks past the cross and turns back to face it before the burden falls** (build 392). The book: "just as Christian came up with the cross, his burden loosed". The build note records the staging; the departures list for Level 5 does not. The moment still reads (he is at the cross), but the fall now waits about a second and a half for a turn.
- **The burden falls while his back is to the sepulchre and rolls away behind him**; he then looks at the cross. Agrees with "he stood still awhile to look" at the cross.
- **"Christian ran" while he walks** (above).
- **The hymn** as the voice of the moment: listed as "the music is ours". It replaces his song, whose last couplet carried the meaning (item 2).
- Listed and fine: dawn; Shining Ones as light; the third one speaking his charge; the child taking the roll and making the leaps.

### (c) Lines

Level 5's words are almost untouched Bunyan. That is close to the book but breaks the owner's own rules for words (plain; at most one old word a line; short): "a place somewhat ascending", "sepulchre", "loosed from off his shoulders", "and so continued, till it came to the mouth of the sepulchre", "He hath given". Four long storyteller lines also describe exactly what the picture shows. Since nothing is recorded yet, this is the cheapest moment to settle them.

|---|---|
| N: "Up the highway between its walls Christian ran, but not without great difficulty, because of the load on his back." | "Up this way Christian ran, but with great difficulty, because of the load on his back." (the wall was named a moment ago in Level 4) |
| N: "He came to a place somewhat ascending, and upon that place stood a cross, and a little below, a sepulchre." | "He came to a rising place. Upon it stood a cross, and a little below, a tomb." |
| N: "Just as Christian came up with the cross, his burden loosed from off his shoulders, and fell from off his back." | "Just as Christian came up to the cross, his burden came loose from his shoulders, and fell from off his back." |
| N: "It began to tumble, and so continued, till it came to the mouth of the sepulchre, where it fell in, and I saw it no more." | "It tumbled down to the mouth of the tomb. There it fell in, and I saw it no more." |
| C: "He hath given me rest by his sorrow, and life by his death." | Keep exactly: this is the level's one famous old line. |
| N: "He stood still to look and wonder. He looked, and looked again, till the tears ran down his cheeks." | "He stood still to look and wonder, that the sight of the cross should ease him of his burden. ### (d) Order

Exactly the book's: glad, his saying, looking and weeping, the Shining Ones, the leaps. No faults.

### (e) Meaning risks

- **The fall reads as grace**: it is automatic, the child does nothing, there is silence and then light. This is the game's best theological staging. Guard it: never add an act here.
- The risk is only in what comes BEFORE: a child who was never told "I cannot get it off myself" ({39}) or "it will fall of itself" ({68}) sees a long climb up a hill and then the load drops, which looks like a reward for reaching the top. The two earlier lines and the "sight of the cross" line together close that gap.
- The Shining Ones' gifts come after the fall and are all given, none earned; the child's only act is to take the roll. Right.

8. **Level 1, DOOR_WORDS**: "He had told them of the danger, but they would not believe him. Now his wife and children saw him run." ({11}, {18}). Mends the hardest picture in the game for a parent and child.

**Pictures and acts that can carry a dropped meaning without words**
