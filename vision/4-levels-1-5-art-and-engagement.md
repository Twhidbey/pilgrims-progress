# Pilgrim's Progress, Levels 1 to 5: artistry and engagement (build 393)

**How this was made.** The game was driven headless (Playwright, 844 x 390, an iPhone held sideways) through all five levels by a script that holds to walk, passes lines and does every act; about 250 stills were saved and the key ones looked at. Nothing in the repo was changed. **I could not hear sound or watch motion**: everything about music, voices and animation below comes from the code and notes, not from ears or eyes. Timings are tick counts from the headless run (60 ticks = 1 s) and include my driver's own short delays before each act; the Level 4 figure also includes picture-loading waits.

- Stills and contact sheets were made in a session scratchpad (not kept in the repo; about 300 stills at 844 x 390). The file names below refer to them; remake any with `tools/stills2.js` / `tools/stills3.js` or the skip links.

## 1. The game as a whole

### What is genuinely beautiful
- **The book frame** (`T-0-cover.png`, `T-1-contents.png`). The painted cover with the tiny pilgrim, the 1678 title page, the contents as levels. It sets the tone at once and is the most "finished" thing in the game. The type (IM Fell, red rules) is the best type in the game.
- **Level 2's oak** (`L2-016-help.png`, `L2-017-help.png`, `L2-019-look.png`, `L2-021-on.png`). Composition, restraint, the mirror, colour coming back as gold on the steps. This is the game's reference picture: everything else should be measured against it.
- **Level 3's opening oak against the low sun** (`L3-000-plain.png`, `L3-005-meet.png`), **the whole mountain** (`L3-012-still.png`, `x3wide.png`) and **the wicket-gate opening** (`L3-030-open.png`). Each would hold a child's eye.
- **Level 1's lit doorway in the rain** (`L1a-000-x5.png`, `L1a-024-x5.png`): the family in the door is the emotional picture of the level.
- **Level 5's sunrise behind the crossing** (`L5-013-gone.png`, `L5-015-shine.png`): simple and right.

### What breaks the spell across levels
1. **Five rendering languages, and they do not meet.** Level 1 is misty flat silhouettes; Level 2 is graphic black and silver; Level 3 mixes a flat code plain, a very detailed painted oak, a near-photographic painted mountain (`x3wide.png`) and a photographed stone wall; Level 4 is realistic oil paintings with faces (`L4-020-r2.run.png`, `L4-088-r6.cage.png`) joined by flat tan code corridors (`L4-016-pass.run.png`) and a schematic doll's-house cutaway (`L4-000-door.run.png`); Level 5 is flat vector shapes (`L5-005-walk.png`). Each is defensible alone; side by side Level 4 looks like a different game, and Level 5 looks like an earlier draft of the same game. The **amount of detail** is the real inconsistency, more than the medium: Level 2 and Level 5 have almost none, Level 3's oak and mountain and Level 4's rooms have a great deal.
2. **Christian himself changes.** Grey watercolour (1 to 3), then a dark oil-painted man with a different build and hood (4), then the watercolour again (5). He is the one constant the child holds onto; in Level 4 he is also no longer steered. The mannequin lab is a possible long-term answer, but today the break is plain.
3. **Scale of the figures swings widely.** Level 1's gate wide shot loses the two men at the foot of a pale tower (`L1-031-x21.png`, `L1-034-x21.png`); Level 4's rooms are shown at about 55% of an already letterboxed picture, so on a phone the palace's people are about 25 px tall and the brave man's fight is a speck (`L4-071-r5.fight.png`), yet in the passages between rooms the two men are three times larger (`L4-032-pass.run.png`). 
4. **Legibility in the dark.** At the foot of the mountain the two men are dark brown on dark brown (`L3-013-come.png`, `L3-016-evan.png`, `L3-017-evan.png`): the kneel and Evangelist's hand, the heart of the level, can hardly be seen even on a bright monitor, and a phone at half brightness will show less. Room 1 before the candle and room 7 in the dream are the same (`L4-008-r1.light.png`, `L4-107-r7.tell.png`), though there the dark is the point.
5. **Letterboxing.** At 844 x 390 the 16:9 picture leaves a dark bar each side, and Level 4 then frames the painting inside that again. Level 4 uses roughly a third of the phone's screen for its picture.

### Does colour and light progress as the story heals (GRIS)?
Partly, and it could be much clearer. Today: L1 blue rain to a pale beige gate; L2 opens gold, goes silver, ends gold; L3 opens in its richest gold-green, goes to dark rose-brown, ends in Rembrandt black and gold; L4 is brown-black throughout; L5 goes from cold violet to a peach dawn. So the game reaches its most colourful moment at the *start of Level 3* (the detour toward Morality), and the Cross's dawn, which should be the first true colour of the game, is paler and flatter than Level 3's opening. The far light, the game's one thread, is strong in L1, present at the end of L2, hidden in L3 (rightly), absent from L4, and returns as the sun in L5: that thread is the best idea in the game and deserves to be made unmissable (see improvements 3 and 9).

### Engagement across the whole
- **The arc of what the child does** is good in 1 to 3 and thins out exactly where the story peaks. L1: walk, touch the light, run, choose, choose, push the gate. L2: walk, fall, choose, wade, take a hand, walk. L3: walk, touch, walk, touch, touch, run, knock. L4: touch, touch, touch (twelve times), watch. L5: one long hold, then about 40 seconds of watching the burden fall, then touch the roll and three taps.
- **The best acts enact the meaning**: touching the light Evangelist points to; pushing the city gate; wading (each step a note of a minor tune, holding less and less); **taking Help's hand** and **Evangelist's hand** (grace as something received); knocking. In Level 4 the broom and the jug (law stirs the dust, the gospel lays it) and the bag of treasure are true to the parable. The weak ones are touches on a thing that only starts the next lines: the candle, the writer's book, the cage (twice), the trembling man, the people on the palace top, the roll.
- **Continuity between levels**: the end cards and "Go on" work; L1 to L2 (through the gate into gold) and L2 to L3 are natural. L3 ends with Good-will in the door and L4 begins at a different door with no gate scene between (the planned Good-will talk is unbuilt): a child will not know why the lit gate they just knocked at is gone. L4 to L5 carries the wall of Salvation in words only; L5's wall is a flat slab (`L5-000-walk.png`). L5's end card says "Next: the Hill Difficulty (not yet built)" (`L5-end.png`): the finish line of the first whole game should not end on a work note.
- **Sound** (from the code): a real strength on paper. The hymn laid as lights in L1, the minor wading tune and drums in L2, the bed of chords and thunder in L3, room moods in L4, the hymn whole at the Cross. The owner's rule to save the hymn for the Cross is the right instinct and makes L5's sound the payoff. But **Level 5 has no recorded voices yet**, so the climax is the only silent-voiced level; that alone will make it feel unfinished to a child used to being read to.
- **Where a 7 to 10 year old would drift**: (a) Level 4, by room 3 (long explanations in adult doctrine: "the work of grace in the heart", "the things which are seen are temporal", plus seven King James verses read aloud in the passages); (b) Level 5's walk up (nearly a minute of holding with two lines and an empty slope); (c) Level 3's foot of the mountain if they cannot see the figures. **Where they would be confused**: why Christian looks different in the house; who the three lights are; where the gate went between 3 and 4; in L5 the tomb is partly behind the word strip just as the burden rolls into it (`L5-011-gone.png`).
- **Where they would feel something**: the wife in the door; "Life! Life!" with fingers in the ears; sinking; the hand; the mountain; the door opening; the sun behind the cross. These are well chosen. The game's emotional skeleton is sound.

## 2. Level by level

### Title and contents
**Art.** Excellent. Cover (`T-0-cover.png`) and opened book (`T-1-contents.png`) are elegant and neutral, as asked. Small points: on the phone the contents lines are about 10 px type (readable, just); "Shew the darker rooms" with a red diamond reads as a sixth chapter rather than a switch, and the old spelling will puzzle a child (a parent's switch, so perhaps fine).
**Engagement.** Touching a book to open it is a lovely first act. The contents let a child jump to any level from the start, which removes any sense of earning the next chapter; not a fault under "no score", but a ribbon or pointing hand that moves on is already there and is enough.

### Level 1, City of Destruction
**Art.**
- Beautiful: the opening (`L1a-000-x5.png`), the family in the door at the choice (`L1a-024-x5.png`), the mockers in lit doorways (`L1-015-x14.png`), the rain easing and warmth entering from the right (`L1-018-x17.png`, `L1-024-x18.png`). The weather progression works and is the game's GRIS idea in miniature.
- The **wheat field does not read as wheat** (`L1a-008-x2.png`, `L1a-011-x2.png`): it is a band of blue-grey mist with faint grass. The notes speak of "a pale wheat field ... wind as bands of light"; in stills it is the least defined place in the level, and it is where the whole story is set going.
- **Christian turns pale and ghostly in the field** beside a dark Evangelist (`L1a-015-x2.png`, `L1a-018-x2.png`): two figures in one light with two different tones, against the owner's "one tint for all figures".
- **The run's first pose** is a low crouch that reads as a hunched dwarf (`L1a-021-x3.png`); the ears-covered run (`L1-008-x7.png`, `L1-010-x9.png`) is much better.
- **The gate is the weakest picture and it is the finale** (`L1-031-x21.png` to `L1-036-x22.png`): a flat beige tower, low contrast against a beige sky, the two men tiny and lost at its foot in the mist, the far light a small dot beside the tower rather than the thing the gate opens onto. The opened arch as a flat cream shape (`L1-034-x21.png`) is the right idea but has no glow, no light on the road, no silhouettes against it. Compare the wicket-gate in L3 (`L3-030-open.png`), which does all of this.
- The little hymn lights scattered in the field and town (`L1a-005-x4.png`, `L1-018-x17.png`) are pretty but sit among window lights and the far light, so three kinds of small warm dot compete.

**Engagement.** The strongest structure in the game: out, then back, with the home door as a real temptation. The two choices matter emotionally and cost nothing. Evangelist's talk is about 9 lines before the act (`L1a-009` to `L1a-017`), over the owner's own 5-line rule; a child will tap through it. The first side message, "Touch the road. The world answers when you touch it." (`L1a-004-x5.png`), is a promise the later levels keep only now and then.

### Level 2, Slough of Despond
**Art.**
- The oak scenes are stunning and consistent: `L2-013-help.png`, `L2-016-help.png`, `L2-017-help.png`, `L2-018-onway.png`, `L2-020-look.png`, `L2-021-on.png`. The steps as warm lights across silver water, with gold returning, is the game's best single use of colour as story.
- The city in the sky (`x2city.png`) is a lovely thought; its hill is a **hard-edged pale half-disc**, which reads as a second sun or a dome.
- **Seams** (the owner's own rule about seams): (1) at the home bank a dark rectangular block fills the bottom-left with a **straight upright edge** where the mud begins (`L2-002-plain.png`, `x2city.png`, bottom left); (2) a soft **upright pale column** stands in the mire right of centre through the whole fall and quarrel (`L2-003-fall.png`, `L2-005-pli.png`, `L2-007-pli.png`, `L2-008-wade.png`): the edge of the mist veil; (3) Help fading in is a **pale ghost on the dark trunk** (`L2-013-help.png`), which build 271 meant to cure.
- **The fears' darkness** (`L2-010-stuck.png`, `x2fear.png`): a rounded window of light with dark all round, banded in level stripes, with a faint dot-grid texture and a hard line down the left edge. It still reads as the "circle of light" the owner turned down, only squarer, and it hides the oak, which is the picture. Christian alone in the wide mire is a small dark lump (`L2-009-wade.png`): fine for loneliness, but for about 20 seconds the picture is nearly empty.
- The mire while they quarrel (`L2-005-pli.png`) is two small shapes in a great pale field; the camera notes say it comes close, yet they are still about a tenth of the picture's height.

**Engagement.** Tight (about 2 minutes), wordless stretches used well, one real choice, one perfect act. The fall is automatic and rightly so. The drums and darkness give a peak; Help's hand resolves it. A child will remember this level. The only slack is the opening walk on the plain (about 20 s) and the last walk (9 s), both short.

### Level 3, Mr. Worldly Wiseman and the hill
**Art.**
- Three great pictures: the oak against the sun (`L3-005-meet.png`), the mountain (`L3-012-still.png`, `x3wide.png`), the gate (`L3-027-knock.png`, `L3-030-open.png`). The level over-delivers on "one stunning picture".
- **The idioms inside the level do not agree.** The plain is a flat gradient with a flat horizon stripe (`L3-008-down.png`) while the oak above it is painted leaf by leaf; the far mountain (`x3hill.png`) is a detailed rock sitting on a flat band of mist with a level line, its village a small yellow-green smudge that does not read as houses; the mountain close up is nearly a photograph. The rocky path (`L3-009-path.png`) is the level's most coherent stretch: pure layered silhouettes, like Level 2.
- **The foot of the mountain is too dark and too brown-on-brown** (`L3-010-path.png`, `L3-011-still.png`, `L3-013-come.png`, `L3-016-evan.png`, `L3-017-evan.png`). The overhang is a great dark mass with a soft inner shape that reads as a smudge; the men are hard to find; the kneel is invisible. Only when the mercy light gathers (`L3-022-evan.png`) does the scene come alive, and then it is beautiful. The mercy light comes about 30 seconds too late for the eye.
- The brow (`L3-007-aside.png`): as he goes over, he is a small blob half-sunk in a low mound; the "going down out of sight" reads more as sinking into a dune.
- At the gate he first appears as a **pale ghost** out of the mist (`L3-026-gate.png`); fine as a fade, but in a still he is lighter than the wall behind him.
- The end card's text lies across the wall and beside the lit door (`L3-031-open.png`): acceptable.

**Engagement.** The best variety of acts and the only real "discoveries" (lizards, hidden notes, the oak's bird, touching cloud and fire in the wide shot). The wide shot is a true reward. The geography (out to the hill, back, mist, gate) asks a lot; the veil of mist hides the join, and a child will simply accept it. The Wiseman talk is now short. Risk: the long dark stretch from the path to "God-speed" (about 1:20 by ticks) in near-black.

### Level 4, the Interpreter's House
**Art.**
- The paintings themselves are accomplished, some lovely: the parlour (`L4-020-r2.run.png`), the fire (`L4-054-r4.run.png`, `L4-058-r4.saw.png`), the cage (`L4-088-r6.cage.png`), the house by night (`L4-004-door.open.png`).
- But they are **shown small, inside a dark frame, inside the letterbox**: about a third of the phone's screen. The palace's whole drama happens among figures about 25 px tall (`L4-066-r5.run.png`, `L4-071-r5.fight.png`). The owner asked for the rooms to feel less claustrophobic; the cure made them miniatures.
- **Three languages in one level**: oil paintings with real faces; flat tan code corridors with large dark figures and a stair of thin lines (`L4-016-pass.run.png`, `L4-079-pass.run.png`, `L4-063-pass.run.png`, where the night garden is a flat navy panel with scalloped shrubs); and the cutaway (`L4-000-door.run.png`), a tidy architectural diagram with a red roof. The corner map in the passages is a cluster of tan boxes (`L4-032-pass.run.png`) that reads as a game HUD, the one thing in the game that looks like a video game.
- **The two visitors stand at the edge of every room, dark, doing nothing** (`L4-037-r3.run.png`, `L4-054-r4.run.png`, `L4-088-r6.cage.png`). In room 1 they stand in front of the room's people, larger than the servant boy (`L4-010-r1.light.png`). The child's hero is a bystander for the whole level.
- Room 7 (`L4-107-r7.tell.png`, `L4-108-r7.tell.png`) is nearly black with a soft orb; the judgment dream, the most dramatic words in the level, has the least to look at.
- Room 1's picture of the grave person is good, but he looks at us, not up to heaven, while the words say "His eyes are lifted up to heaven" (`L4-010-r1.light.png`; already noted as a departure).

**On the question being weighed (paintings or code-drawn rooms).** From the stills: the paintings are individually the richer pictures, but they are the cause of every break listed above (a second Christian, real faces, no movement, a frame within a frame, rooms that cannot be composed around the two men, corridors that cannot match). Code-drawn rooms in the language of Level 2 and Level 3's gate (few things, one light, soft pools, silhouettes) would bring back the game's own Christian, full-screen pictures, and control of scale. The gate of Level 3 already proves that a code-drawn Rembrandt interior light can be stunning (`L3-030-open.png`). My reading: **go to code-drawn rooms, and let each master survive as the light and one quoted object**, as the mood board first proposed. The risk is that plain code rooms look thin (the passages do); the gate, not the passages, is the standard to hold them to.

**Engagement.** The level is the longest by far (the notes' own measure: 4:45, or 6:30 with the darker rooms; over a third of the game) and the least played. It is a gallery walk: stand, listen, touch the ringed thing, listen. Specific points:
- **The parlour and Passion's treasure are the two that work**, because the touch causes something a child understands (dust flies and chokes; water lays it; gold turns to bubbles and rags).
- **The fire** has the level's best idea (go round the wall and see who feeds it) but as built the far side is in the same picture, so "going round" is a hold while a dark panel lifts (`L4-058-r4.saw.png`). A discovery became a reveal.
- **The palace**: the child touches the writer's book, then watches a 25 px man win. The meaning ("set down my name": courage, enlisting) is close to the child's act but the fight should be the child's too.
- **The passages** add seven read-aloud King James verses ("We must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God"). They are apt for an adult; for a child they are seven more waits between rooms, in the level already longest on words. The storyteller's rule ("only what is relevant to the story") arguably applies.
- Language for a 7 to 10 year old: "the work of grace in the heart", "temporal ... eternal", "pretend to lead you right, but their way goes down to death", "the Son of the Blessed". Each room's meaning takes two or three such lines.
- The cutaway of the house is a good orienting idea and a child will like seeing the rooms light up at the end; it is the one "progress" picture that is not a score.

### Level 5, the Cross
**Art.**
- The idea is right and the dawn works: `L5-013-gone.png`, `L5-015-shine.png`. Emptiness after Level 3's fury and Level 4's dark is the correct contrast.
- **But it is still the concept sketch.** The slope is one flat dark shape with a smooth vector edge (`L5-002-walk.png`, `L5-005-walk.png`); the far ridges are two flat bands; the cross is two thin black bars with square ends (`L5-007-cross.png`), nearer a plus sign than "one small plain rough cross"; the wall of Salvation is a flat slab with faint lines that ends in a square corner (`L5-000-walk.png`); the tomb is a dark mound. Level 2's oak and Level 3's gate show how much life the same flat language can carry; here nothing has yet been given that care. Before dawn the whole picture is two near-equal violets with very little to look at for a minute.
- **A dotted line** runs along the crown where the lit rim should be (`L5-013-gone.png`, `L5-015-shine.png`): a seam fault.
- **The tomb is behind the words** exactly when the burden rolls in (`L5-009-tumble.png`, `L5-011-gone.png`): the caption strip covers the hollow, so "it fell in, and I saw it no more" cannot be seen.
- **The Shining Ones** are three pale lozenges (`L5-020-shine.png`): candle flames more than persons, small, with a tiny roll with a red dot between them and the cross. "Awe first" is not met.
- **His new clothes** are a tint; the "mark" is a dot. After five levels with the burden, the figure without it (`L5-015-shine.png`) is a small dark upright shape; the change the whole game has waited for is hard to feel in his outline at this size.
- Colour: the dawn is a peach wash. It is warmer than before, but it is less saturated than Level 3's opening plain and less golden than Level 2's ending. The first true dawn of the game should out-shine both.

**Engagement.**
- The walk up is about 55 seconds of holding with two lines of words and nothing to touch or see change except a slow warming (`L5-000` to `L5-006`). Friedrich's emptiness is right for a painting; for a child's thumb it is the game's longest dead stretch, placed just before its peak.
- **The climax is watched, not done.** From the stop at the cross to the first Shining One's words the child does nothing for about 40 seconds. The book requires the burden to fall by itself, and that is right; but everything round it could be the child's.
- The acts that remain (touch the roll, three taps to leap) are slight. The leaps are a hop of a standing picture in code.
- No voices yet; "Next: the Hill Difficulty (not yet built)" on the last card.
- What is right: silence before the hymn; the hymn whole and in tune here for the first time (the best long-range idea in the game's sound); the sun exactly behind the crossing.

## 3. Improvements, ranked

Everything below is free (code only) unless marked. Nothing here proposes: crisp or photographic smoke, bright multi-green or cartoon trees, collecting or counters, code "breathing" in the house, more Amazing Grace before the Cross, grass or clutter on Level 2's shores, tall flames on the hill, a circle of light round Christian, or anything that moves him without the child.

### Quick wins (an hour or two each)
1. **Level 5: lift the words off the tomb.** During the tumble, raise the camera or the tomb's ground so the sepulchre's mouth is above the word strip, and hold the caption until the bundle is inside (`L5-011-gone.png`). The single most important beat of five levels is currently half hidden.
2. **Level 5: fix the dotted rim on the crown** and give the cross a little roughness (uneven hewn edges, a slight lean, pegged joint) so it is a thing, not a symbol (`L5-013-gone.png`, `L5-007-cross.png`).
3. **Level 5: make dawn the child's act, echoing Level 1.** After the silence, let the faint light behind the cross be touched ("touch the light", the very act Evangelist asked for in the field); the touch brings the sun up and starts the hymn. The burden still falls by itself, as the book says; what the child does is look to the light, which is the story's meaning. It also ties the far light of Level 1 to the Cross without a word.
4. **Level 5: end card.** Replace "Next: the Hill Difficulty (not yet built)" with a true ending for now (for example the book's own "and went on singing"), and offer the contents.
5. **Level 3: bring the mercy-light forward, faintly.** Lift the men off the rock at the foot (a paler low mist behind them, as build 314 did on the path, carried to the foot; or the men a tone lighter there) so the kneel and the offered hand can be seen before the touch (`L3-016-evan.png`, `L3-017-evan.png`). Then the full mercy light is still the reward.
6. **Level 2: three seams.** Soften or hide the upright edge of the dark block under the home bank (`L2-002-plain.png`); feather the mist veil's left edge so no pale column stands in the mire (`L2-005-pli.png`); keep Help dark as he fades in against the trunk (`L2-013-help.png`).
7. **Level 2: the fears' dark.** Lose the bright window: make the dark mist truly uneven (thicker drifts crossing the sky, the oak's limb still faintly there), remove the dot-grid texture and the hard left line (`x2fear.png`, `L2-010-stuck.png`).
8. **Level 2: the city's hill.** Soften the half-disc under the city in the sky into haze so only spire and roofs float in the gold (`x2city.png`).
9. **Level 1: the gate's finale.** Borrow from Level 3's wicket-gate: when the doors part, let warm light pour low and flat onto the wet road, put the two men dark against it, deepen the tower to a darker, nearer tone, and bring the camera a little closer so they are not specks (`L1-034-x21.png` beside `L3-030-open.png`). The far light should be seen *through* the arch.
10. **Level 1: one tone for the two men in the field** (Christian is pale, Evangelist dark: `L1a-015-x2.png`), and check the first running pose (`L1a-021-x3.png`).
11. **Level 4: drop or shorten the read-aloud verses in the passages** (show them silently as before build 315, or keep one, at the last passage). This removes seven waits from the longest level. Owner's call: they asked for them.
12. **Level 4: remove the corner map from the passages** (the HUD-like boxes, `L4-032-pass.run.png`); keep the whole cutaway at the door and on leaving, where it is a picture.
13. **Level 4: show the rooms larger** while they remain paintings: fill the picture's width and let the visitors stand in the dark margin (build 331's idea without the shrink), so the palace's people are twice the size.

### Medium moves (a day or so each, still free)
14. **Give Level 5 the care Level 2's oak got.** Same flat language, more life: the slope's edge broken by grass tufts only at the crown's rim where the light catches (sparingly), three or four more hazed ridges so the dawn has depth to travel through, mist lying in the far hollows that lifts as the sun rises, the wall as real coursed stones ending in a tumbled end, a tomb with a cut stone mouth. And push the dawn's colour: after the sun, let green come into the hill and blue into the high sky, so this is visibly the most coloured moment of the game so far.
15. **Level 5: shorten and enrich the climb.** Cut the walk by a third; lay two or three quiet things on it that answer a touch (in the game's own habit): the wall's last stones, a lark going up as the sky warms, the burden's creak growing heavier and his pace slowing as he nears the top (holding does less and less, as on the wrong path in Level 3, so the release is felt in the thumb as well as the eye).
16. **Level 5: after the burden falls, the walk is light.** Let the child hold again and feel him move freely (quicker, upright, no creak in the step sound) for a few paces toward the Shining Ones, instead of standing through the hymn. The contrast in the child's own hand is the feeling of the level. (A free clip of him walking without the burden is already listed as wanted.)
17. **Level 5: the Shining Ones as persons of light.** Taller than him, clearly three figures with heads and long garments in the 'lighter' layer, each stepping forward for his own gift (the first lifts a hand; the second's light wraps him; the third holds out the roll), so the three gifts are three beats the child can follow.
18. **Level 1: make the wheat field a picture.** It is where the story starts and where Evangelist stands; today it is mist. The notes' own description (pale crop in three layers, wind crossing as bands of light, thinning into mist at the far end) would make it the level's second picture.
