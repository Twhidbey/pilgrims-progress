# Awe (e): nature that points to God's power and mercy

Researcher (e) for the awe brief (`vision/awe-brief.md`). One beautiful sight of the made world per level, 1 to 16, each grounded in Bunyan's own words or in a Scripture he cites in his margin, after the master already chosen for that level. **Nothing in the game was changed, nothing was spent.** The book was read directly; section numbers `{N}` and margin references are quoted from `book/pilgrims-progress-part-one.txt`. Departures are marked **[departure]**.

## The idea in a sentence

Bunyan almost never describes nature for its own sake, so when he does it matters: a **spring** at the foot of the hard hill, a **window toward the sun-rising**, a land **seen far off from a roof**, a **morning after the darkest night** ("He hath turned the shadow of death into the morning", Amos 5:8), a **river with green trees and lilies** where pilgrims "might lie down safely", **mountains with orchards and flocks**, and a country where "the sun shineth night and day" and "the voice of the turtle" is heard. Each is a gift given **after** a hardship. So the rule for this theme: **the sight comes after the trouble, wordless, the camera rests, the child may touch one small living thing, and nothing is explained.** Mercy is shown, not told.

## Four rules for every sight (from CLAUDE.md, made specific)

1. **A held breath, not a scene.** The level's story stops for 4 to 8 seconds; the camera eases back or up; no line is said (or one short line of Bunyan's, after the pause). The child can walk on whenever they like (the hold walks on as usual; nothing is lost if they do). This is a camera rest, a "story move", which the rules allow.
2. **One special thing.** The sight IS the level's one special thing, or it deepens the level's one picture; it never sits beside another.
3. **The touch is small and alive.** A bird lifts, water rings, a lamb looks up, a leaf turns. It answers once and is quiet; no glint chase, no counting. Touches only while no act is waiting (as Level 3's eggs).
4. **Sound is the world's own.** Free recordings (CC0 / public domain: birdsong, water, wind) or the game's own made sounds; music only where the brief allows (one remembered phrase of the hymn at three moments of grace after the Cross; none of them is spent here unless noted).

## The sixteen sights

Cost is free unless marked. "Touch" is gentle and optional. "Sound" names a free source to look for first (OpenGameArt CC0, Wikimedia Commons, xeno-canto has some CC0 but mostly BY-NC: check each).

### 1. City of Destruction: the rain stops at the gate (deepen)
- **WHERE:** after the gate is pushed open, before he walks through (`{24}` region; the gate is ours).
- **SEE:** the rain thins and stops in the space of a breath; the last drops fall from the gate's arch; the far light stands clear for the first time, small and steady; puddles on the road still and mirror it.
- **TOUCH:** a puddle: one ring spreads, the mirrored light wavers and settles.
- **HEAR:** the rain's hiss fading to drip, drip; nothing else.
- **WHY:** the level's weather already runs rain to gold (`GOLD_MAX`); this makes the turn a moment. The book has no weather here **[departure, small]**; it rhymes with Psalm 147:8 only loosely, so do not quote a verse.
- **RISK:** low. Clutter: none added. Do not add a rainbow (see 13).

### 2. Slough of Despond: the still water mirrors the light (deepen)
- **WHERE:** after Help draws him out and "set him upon sound ground" (`{32}`; margin Ps. 40:2, "He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock").
- **SEE:** the mud's churn settles; under the oak's long limb the standing water goes glass-still and, for the first time in the level, holds the far light's reflection (the level's rule "a surface is either mud or water" is kept: it is the water near the far bank).
- **TOUCH:** the water: a ring, the light wavers, comes back.
- **HEAR:** two or three slow drips from his robe; a far curlew-like call **[departure: no bird in the book here]**, or nothing.
- **WHY:** Ps. 40:2 is Bunyan's own margin; the stillness after the miry clay is the verse made picture.
- **RISK:** the level is already long; make it a 3-second rest inside the existing `look` phase, no new line.

### 3. Worldly Wiseman and the hill: leave the oak as it is
- The level's one picture is the oak against the low sun and then Martin's mountain. Adding nature would cut against the terror it needs. **Only:** the oak's easter-egg bird (`oakTouch`, `OBIRD`) could fly right, toward where the gate will be, instead of left. Free.

### 4. The Interpreter's House: one first star (optional)
- **WHERE:** leaving, "the highway ... fenced on either side with a wall ... called Salvation" (`{92}`).
- **SEE:** as he steps onto the highway, a single star stands above the wall's far end where the first grey of morning will come.
- **RISK:** clutter in a long level; take it only if the leaving shot looks empty. Departure.

### 5. The Cross: one bird after the hymn (deepen)
- **WHERE:** after "Blest cross!" and the three leaps (`{94}`), as the end card waits.
- **SEE:** the mist in the hollows lifting with the sun; a lark rising small over the crown of the hill.
- **TOUCH:** the lark: it climbs higher, singing, and is lost in the light.
- **HEAR:** a skylark recording (look on Commons for public domain / CC0 first). The first birdsong of the game.
- **WHY:** "glad and lightsome ... with a merry heart" (`{94}`). The lark is ours **[departure]**. It sets up Beulah's "singing of birds" (`{382}`): the first and last birdsong in the game rhyme.
- **RISK:** it must come after the Shining Ones have gone, never during them.

### 6. Hill Difficulty: the spring at the foot (NEW, Friedrich)
- **WHERE:** `{103}`: "at the bottom of which was a spring ... Christian now went to the spring, and drank thereof, to refresh himself" (margin Isa. 49:10: "neither shall the heat nor sun smite them: for he that hath mercy on them shall lead them, even by the springs of water shall he guide them").
- **SEE:** at the foot of the tall pale hill, a small clear pool among rocks, the morning sky and the thin line of the way mirrored in it; he kneels (the kneel pack, coat version once it exists) and drinks.
- **TOUCH (the act):** the water: cupped hands, a ring, a soft chime. The child gives him the drink before the climb.
- **HEAR:** a trickle (OpenGameArt CC0 water packs, already used for the bog).
- **LATER IN THE SAME LEVEL:** "the sun went down upon Christian" (`{108}`); the sky is the level's clock (README plan). As he reaches the top in the dark, the **first stars** come out over the Palace's lit window: the tray's roll found, the day gone, and yet the stars. Wordless.
- **RISK:** low; it is the book's own act.

### 7. Palace Beautiful: the window toward the sun-rising, and the land seen far off (NEW, Rembrandt then Friedrich)
- **WHERE:** `{131}` "a large upper chamber, whose window opened towards the sun-rising: the name of the chamber was Peace; where he slept till break of day"; `{137}` "they had him to the top of the house, and bid him look south ... at a great distance, he saw a most pleasant mountainous country, beautified with woods, vineyards, fruits of all sorts, flowers also, with springs and fountains, very delectable to behold" (margin Isa. 33:16, 17: "Thine eyes shall see the king in his beauty: they shall behold the land that is very far off").
- **SEE:** (a) in the dark chamber the window slowly fills with the first sunrise, the one light of the room, a flat pool on the floor creeping toward the bed (the child need do nothing). (b) On the roof: figures from behind at a parapet (Friedrich), ridge beyond ridge to the south, the far light above them. This is the **promise** shot; Level 14 is its fulfilment, so draw Level 14's ridges and show them here first, tiny and hazed.
- **TOUCH:** on the roof, the far mountains: a breeze runs across the near trees and the haze thins a little for a breath, then closes again ("at a great distance").
- **HEAR:** dawn: one blackbird or robin, very far.
- **WHY:** both are Bunyan's own; Isa. 33:17 is the theme of the whole second half (the land very far off, seen, then reached).
- **RISK:** two sights in one level: (a) is a room's light, (b) is the level's picture. If one must go, keep (b).

### 8. Valley of Humiliation: the leaves of the tree of life (deepen the plan)
- **WHERE:** `{154}` "Then there came to him a hand, with some of the leaves of the tree of life, the which Christian took, and applied to the wounds ... and was healed immediately."
- **SEE:** after the battle, the picture dark and grey-red; a hand of light (Shining One style) out of the right edge holding three leaves, the only green in the picture. When the child lays them on him, **green flows back into the valley from where he sits**, outward (GRIS's colour returning), and it is afternoon.
- **TOUCH (the act, already in the Level 8 plan):** the leaves.
- **HEAR:** wind in grass returning; no music.
- **WHY:** the book's own; the tree of life is the same tree as the River of Life's (Rev. 22:2, "the leaves of the tree were for the healing of the nations"), so the green here and the green at Level 12 should be the same green.
- **RISK:** none beyond the plan.

### 9. Valley of the Shadow: the seven stars and Orion, then the morning (NEW, the level's turn)
- **WHERE:** `{164}` "And by and by the day broke; then said Christian, He hath turned 'the shadow of death into the morning'" (margin Amos 5:8). The whole verse: **"Seek him that maketh the seven stars and Orion, and turneth the shadow of death into the morning."** Then `{165}` the look back; `{166}` "His candle shineth upon my head" (Job 29:3).
- **SEE:** near the end of the black valley, the walls open for the first time onto sky: the Pleiades and Orion, sharp, the only lights since the level began, and low on the far side the first grey. A held breath. Then the grey becomes the sunrise the plan already calls the most beautiful sky so far, and the look back.
- **TOUCH:** none needed. If any: a touch on a star makes it brighten slightly and the next one along the belt answers (three soft high notes, never the hymn).
- **HEAR:** the whispers of the valley stop; silence; then one far bird at the first light.
- **WHY:** it is Bunyan's own citation, read to its first half: the God who made the stars is the God who turns the night to morning. It gives the darkest level a single picture of power before mercy. Stars as they are, nothing invented.
- **RISK:** the level's darkness rule (Whistler, near-black): the gap of sky must stay small and come late. Sketch: `concept/awe/nature-seven-stars-and-orion.jpg`.

### 10. Faithful: two shadows on a long road
- **WHERE:** the long talk on the road (`{169}` on).
- **SEE:** late gold light (Hasui), the two figures' long shadows side by side on the road; a pair of birds crossing the great sky together, far up.
- **TOUCH:** the birds: they wheel once and go on.
- **WHY:** the book gives no landscape here; the companionship is the book's **[departure: the birds]**. Keep it a 3-second rest at the start, before Talkative.
- **RISK:** clutter; low priority.

### 11. Vanity Fair: the one clean sky
- **WHERE:** the trial and Faithful's chariot (`{242}`, "a chariot and a couple of horses, waiting ... carried up through the clouds").
- **SEE:** the only nature in the fair is above it: over the lanterns the sky has been smoke; as the chariot rises, the smoke parts on clear evening blue and one star. Not a separate sight: it is the chariot shot's sky.
- **RISK:** nothing to add; just make sure the fair's sky is never lovely until then.

### 12. The River of Life (NEW, Corot; THE sight of the second half)
- **WHERE:** `{276}` "a pleasant river; which David the king called 'the river of God', but John, 'the river of the water of life' [Ps. 65:9, Rev. 22, Ezek. 47] ... on the banks of this river, on either side, were green trees, that bore all manner of fruit; and the leaves of the trees were good for medicine ... On either side of the river was also a meadow, curiously beautified with lilies, and it was green all the year long. In this meadow they lay down, and slept; for here they might lie down safely ... Thus they did several days and nights." (margin Ps. 23:2, "He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters"; Isa. 14:30).
- **SEE:** evening; the far bank's trees mirrored whole in the still water; the near meadow with lilies; the two men lie down in it. Then the held breath that is the level's heart: **while they sleep, days and nights pass in the sky and the water**: evening gold, a night of stars mirrored in the river, a pale morning, gold again; two or three turns, perhaps 20 seconds, wordless. They wake, eat, drink, go on. After the silver mine and Lot's wife (grey and white), this is the first green of the second half.
- **TOUCH:** the water (a ring, the mirrored trees waver); a tree (one fruit drops into the grass, he picks it up); a lily (it closes as night comes, opens at morning, only if touched). Each once.
- **HEAR:** the river's soft run; at night, crickets; at dawn, one bird. **The one remembered phrase of Amazing Grace** (one of the three allowed after the Cross) could sound here, as they lie down: "I once was lost, but now am found" is the right phrase. Owner's choice.
- **WHY:** Bunyan's richest nature passage before Beulah; Ps. 23:2 is his own margin and Psalm 23 already belongs to the Valley (Level 9): "the valley of the shadow" then "still waters" is the psalm in order.
- **RISK:** the time-lapse must not feel like a game timer: slow, no counter, the child can wake them early by holding to walk (nothing lost). Sketch: `concept/awe/nature-river-of-life.jpg`.

### 13. By-path Meadow and Doubting Castle: the storm, then a Sunday morning
- **WHERE:** `{280}` "it began to rain, and thunder, and lighten in a very dreadful manner; and the water rose amain"; the escape at `{292}` before dawn on Sunday, the Giant's fits "in sunshiny weather" (`{284}`).
- **SEE:** after the key called Promise opens the last gate, they come out into a **washed morning**: every blade of grass and the castle's dark stones beaded with light, the flood water lying in the meadow as standing water now mirroring a clean sky.
- **TOUCH:** a dripping branch: it shakes down a small rain of lit drops.
- **A RAINBOW?** It is tempting (Genesis 9:13, the bow as promise, after a flood). It is **not in Bunyan** and would be a loud second light; I advise **against**, or at most a very faint short arc low over the far hills, only if the owner wants one rainbow in the whole game. If ever, here and nowhere else (flood, promise, key called Promise all line up). **[departure]**
- **RISK:** the dungeon's single slit of light is the level's picture; the washed morning is its answer, keep it short.

### 14. The Delectable Mountains (NEW picture, Samuel Palmer)
- **WHERE:** `{295}` "they went up to the mountains, to behold the gardens and orchards, the vineyards and fountains of water; where also they drank and washed themselves ... Now there were on the tops of these mountains Shepherds feeding their flocks"; `{296}` "These mountains are Immanuel's Land ... the sheep also are his, and he laid down his life for them" (John 10:11); `{299}` "a pleasant prospect on every side".
- **SEE:** ridge beyond ridge, pale gold far to olive near, more layers than any level; orchard trees in rows on a middle slope; the flock as pale flecks; the shepherds and pilgrims small on the near hilltop, the far light above the mist. It is the land promised from the Palace roof (Level 7), now walked in.
- **TOUCH:** the flock: the nearest sheep look up and drift a few steps; one lamb bleats. A fountain (if drawn): it rings.
- **HEAR:** sheep bells far off; wind; a thin shepherd's pipe is tempting **[departure]**, better a bird.
- **WHY:** Bunyan's own; the sheep line (John 10:11) lets nature carry the gospel without a sermon: the child sees the flock first, then the Shepherd's word says whose they are.
- **RISK:** the Shepherds' "wonders" (Error, Caution, the byway to hell) are the level's dark part: keep this sight for the arrival, before them, so the beauty comes first. Sketch: `concept/awe/nature-delectable-mountains.jpg`.

### 15. The Flatterer and the Enchanted Ground: the haze lifting
- **WHERE:** as Hopeful's story ends and they come to the edge of the ground (`{381}`/`{382}`).
- **SEE:** the warm drowsy haze clears from the top down (already in the Level 15 plan); the first thing seen sharp is a sky with the far light close; the first faint birdsong of Beulah heard before Beulah is seen.
- **RISK:** none; this is a transition, not a sight. Do not add anything.

### 16. Beulah: the voice of the turtle (NEW, Turner)
- **WHERE:** `{382}` "here they heard continually the singing of birds, and saw every day the flowers appear on the earth, and heard the voice of the turtle in the land [Isa. 62:4, Song of Solomon 2:10-12]. In this country the sun shineth night and day"; `{384}` "orchards, vineyards, and gardens, and their gates opened into the highway ... They are the King's, and are planted here for his own delight, and also for the solace of pilgrims".
- **SEE:** every colour the game has withheld, uncapped (`GOLD_MAX` 1); flowers opening in the near grass as they walk (Song 2:12, "The flowers appear on the earth"); orchards; a sun that does not set (the light never changes for the whole level, the only level like that); the City a brightness over the water. **The one bird of the level is a turtle-dove** on a branch by the way: small, grey, still.
- **TOUCH:** the dove: it calls, a soft "roo-roo" (Song 2:12, "the voice of the turtle is heard in our land"). Explain nowhere that "turtle" means dove; a child will simply see a dove.
- **HEAR:** birdsong, continual (the book says continually): a bed of free birdsong recordings at low level, the only level with a bird bed.
- **WHY:** Bunyan's own, with Song 2:10-12 in the margin ("Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away. For, lo, the winter is past, the rain is over and gone"): **the rain of Level 1's first screen is "over and gone"**. That is the whole game's weather in one verse.
- **RISK:** Turner's dissolve must not become busy; flowers few, one dove, light doing the work.

## My top three

1. **The River of Life with days and nights passing while they sleep (Level 12, `{276}`, Ps. 23:2).** The book's most beautiful nature passage, a wordless held breath of 20 seconds, the stars mirrored in still water, and the place for one remembered phrase of the hymn. It is mercy as rest, the opposite of every level that made him hurry. Sketch drawn.
2. **The seven stars and Orion before the morning (Level 9, `{164}`, Amos 5:8).** Bunyan cites the verse; the first half of it gives the darkest level one picture of God's power (he made the stars) just before the mercy of the morning. Costs nothing, adds no words, and it is real sky. Sketch drawn.
3. **The voice of the turtle in Beulah (Level 16, `{382}`, Song 2:10-12).** One dove the child can touch, birdsong continual, a sun that never sets, and the margin's "the rain is over and gone" closing the game's weather arc from Level 1. With the lark at the Cross (Level 5), the first and last birdsong rhyme.

Close behind: the spring at the foot of Hill Difficulty (Level 6, Isa. 49:10, the child gives him the drink), and the land seen far off from the Palace roof (Level 7, Isa. 33:17) as the promise Level 14's Palmer mountains keep.

## A thread across the game (so the sights add up, not pile up)

- **Water:** the miry clay (2) → the still water that mirrors the light (2) → the spring (6) → the River of Life (12) → the flood and the washed morning (13) → the fountains on the mountains (14) → the River of death (16), crossed. Each mirror is clearer than the last.
- **Birds:** none in the rain of Level 1's city (only the field's birds) → the oak's one bird (3) → the lark at the Cross (5) → a dawn bird after the Valley (9) → crickets and a dawn bird at the river (12) → sheep and a bird on the mountains (14) → continual birdsong and the turtle-dove (16).
- **Sky:** rain (1) → the first dawn (5) → the first stars (6) → the window toward the sun-rising (7) → Orion and the morning (9) → nights mirrored in the river (12) → a sun that never sets (16).
- **Green:** none until the leaves of the tree of life (8) bring it back, the same green as the river's trees (12); full colour only in Beulah (16).
