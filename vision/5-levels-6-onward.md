## 0. Big recommendations first

1. **Sixteen levels, not seventeen: merge 15 and 16.** Ignorance, the Flatterer, Atheist and the Enchanted Ground are, in the book, almost all talk (about 800 lines of conversation, perhaps 60 lines of event). The playable beats are four: the net, the man who laughs, staying awake, Ignorance falling behind. One level, "The Flatterer and the Enchanted Ground".
2. **Move the lions from Level 6 to open Level 7.** The lions lie "about a furlong off the porter's lodge" and the porter Watchful speaks during them (`{110}`). Level 6 then ends on its strongest image: night falling, and "behold there was a very stately palace before him, the name of which was Beautiful" (`{109}`). Level 7 opens with the lions as Level 4 opened with the gate.
3. **End Level 12 at the River of the Water of Life** (`{276}`): the rest in the meadow of lilies is the breath before By-path Meadow, and Level 13 opens where "the river and the way for a time parted" (`{277}`).
4. **Bunyan's own interval.** At `{306}` he writes "So I awoke from my dream" and at `{307}` "And I slept, and dreamed again". Between Levels 14 and 15 the book on the title page can close and open again: one quiet card, "And I slept, and dreamed again." It is in the book, costs nothing, and makes a natural resting place (a second "finish line" after the Cross: Levels 6 to 14).
5. **Reuse the "Shew the darker rooms" switch** (`pp-dark`) for the whole second half: one switch, "Shew the darker places", turns on the standard telling of the dark parts; off, the gentle telling. Each level below gives both.
6. **Three assets decide the cost of everything after the Cross**, more than any single level:
   - **Christian without the burden** (walk, stand, turn, kneel, run): every pack he has carries it. Paint it out free as the book was (build 189), or free clips, or the mannequin (`MQ`), which was built for exactly this ("other figures as other clothes on the same skeleton"). Decide this once, before Level 6.
   - **Faithful** (walk, stand; Levels 10, 11, a glimpse in 17).
   - **Hopeful** (walk, stand, a wading clip; Levels 11 to 17): the most used new figure in the game.
7. **Music after the Cross.** Amazing Grace was the tune of seeking, found note by note and made whole at the Cross; the owner's rule is not to overplay it. Proposal: it is **silent from Level 6 to 16** except as one remembered phrase at three moments of grace (the roll found again, the key called Promise, "I feel the bottom, and it is good"), and comes whole a second and last time at the Celestial Gate, played by the child on the King's trumpets and the city's bells. For the road between, a **second, walking theme**: Bunyan wrote a song after nearly every episode, so Christian "went on singing" (`{94}`) is the book's own device. Candidates for its tune, all old and free: Tallis's Canon, Old Hundredth, or the English folk tune under "He who would valiant be" (Bunyan's own words, from Part Two; the folk melody is traditional, but the well-known 1906 arrangement "Monks Gate" may still be in copyright in places: use the plain folk tune, or check). Psalm 23 for the Valley: the tune "Crimond" (1872).

- **Light.** Levels 1 to 5 went from rain to the first dawn. Levels 6 to 16 go through three nights (Palace, Valley, Castle), each ending in a better morning, to the land where there is no night. The far light of Level 1 is glimpsed three times on the way (from the Palace roof, `{137}`; through the glass, `{305}`; from Beulah, `{383}`) and at the end turns out to be the City itself.
- **Companions.** Alone (6 to 9); the voice of Faithful in the dark before he is seen (9); Faithful (10, 11); Hopeful "rises out of his ashes" (`{244}`) and stays to the end. In the River the roles turn over: Christian, who led, sinks; Hopeful holds his head up.
- **Hands.** The game's acts of mercy have been hands: Help's (2), Evangelist's (3), Good-will's (4). Carry it on: Faithful helps Christian up (10, `{170}`); Hopeful "joined himself unto him" (11); the Shepherds "took them by the hand" (14, `{298}`); Hopeful in the River (16); the Shining Ones "lead them up by the arms" (16, `{394}`).
- **The roll.** Read for comfort (6), lost in sleep and found (6), carried unseen for ten levels, glinting in the tray whenever he is afraid, and given in at the gate (`{399}`): the tray's whole reason pays off in the last minute of the game. Ignorance "fumbled in his bosom for one, and found none" (`{403}`).
- **The key.** Suggestion: from the Cross on, a small key lies in the tray beside the roll, never glinting, never explained. In the dungeon: "I have a Key in my bosom called Promise" (`{292}`). He, and the child, had it all along.
- **Callbacks that cost nothing:** fingers in his ears at Vanity Fair (`{222}`) reuse Level 1's ears run; "Remember Lot's Wife" (`{272}`) answers Level 1's "Stop and look back"; the River reuses Level 2's wading and stuck clips and its darkness ("the River can reuse both", build 214); the iron cage of the Interpreter's house returns as the cage in the fair; the door in the hill (14) is where Ignorance is taken (16); the hill of the City is climbed "with ease" (`{394}`) where Hill Difficulty was hands and knees.

## Level 6. The Hill Difficulty (`{95}` to `{109}`)

**Beats, in order.** Three men asleep in fetters, Simple, Sloth, Presumption; he tries to wake them (`{95}`, `{96}`) → Formalist and Hypocrisy tumble over the wall (`{97}`-`{102}`) → the foot of the hill, a spring, three ways: Difficulty straight up, Danger to the left, Destruction to the right (`{103}`, `{104}`) → he drinks and climbs: "from running to going, and from going to clambering upon his hands and his knees" (`{105}`) → the arbour midway; he reads the roll, looks at his coat, sleeps till almost night; the roll falls from his hand; one wakes him (`{105}`) → the top: Timorous and Mistrust run back from the lions (`{106}`) → he feels for the roll and finds it not; kneels; goes back, weeping (`{107}`, `{108}`) → finds it under the settle (`{109}`) → up again nimbly, but the sun goes down → the Palace Beautiful ahead.

**Cut:** the argument with Formalist and Hypocrisy about law and the coat (`{98}`-`{101}`) to two lines; the long self-reproach (`{107}`, `{108}`) to one. **Must stay:** the three sleepers' three answers; the three ways; the sleep and the lost roll; "I must venture"; the roll found.

**Key lines.**
- N: "He saw three men fast asleep, with fetters upon their heels." (✓ `{95}`)
- C: "Awake, and come away! I will help you off with your irons." (✓ `{96}`)
- Simple: "I see no danger." Sloth: "Yet a little more sleep." Presumption: "Every tub must stand upon its own bottom." (✓; "fat" made "tub")
- N: "Two men came tumbling over the wall. Their names were Formalist and Hypocrisy." (✓ `{97}`)
- C: "Why did you not come in at the gate?" / F: "It was too far about. We took a short cut, as all our people do." (`{98}`, `{99}`; check)
- N: "The narrow way lay right up the hill, and the name of it is Difficulty." (✓ `{103}`)
- C: "Better, though difficult, the right way to go, than wrong, though easy, where the end is woe." (✓ his verse, `{103}`)
- N: "He fell from running to going, and from going to clambering upon his hands and his knees." (✓ `{105}`)
- N: "He fell into a fast sleep; and in his sleep, his roll fell out of his hand." (✓)
- A voice: "Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways and be wise." (✓; the one old line kept)
- Timorous: "The further we go, the more danger we meet with." Mistrust: "Just before us lie a couple of lions in the way!" (✓ `{106}`)

- C: "O, that I had not slept!" (✓ `{108}`)

**The picture.** After **Caspar David Friedrich's mountain pictures** (*The Watzmann*, *Morning in the Riesengebirge*), composed like a tall scroll: the game's first level that travels **upward**. One great pale hill in three or four tones filling the frame; the way a thin line straight up it; the two easy ways curling off left and right at its foot into a dark wood and into "a wide field, full of dark mountains" (`{104}`); the arbour a small roofed seat halfway, the one man-made thing. The one light is the sun itself, travelling: high as he drinks, low and gold in the arbour, gone as he finds the roll, so the lost time is seen in the sky. Palette: clear cool greys and greens of an upland day, to rose, to deep blue; the Palace's first lit window is the last thing seen.
- *The flat-feet rule* ("people stand and walk only on level ground or a very gentle rise"): build the hill as level shelves joined by short steep faces (as the Slough's banks), the camera rising with him; the clamber is one new clip on a short face.

**The child's acts.**
1. Touch each sleeper: each stirs, says his line, and settles again. (Nothing the child does wakes them: that is the meaning.)
2. Touch the spring: he drinks (`{103}`).
3. Hold to climb; holding moves him less and less (as on the wrong path in Level 3, but here it is the right one), run, walk, hands and knees.
4. In the arbour, touch the roll in the tray: it opens close up (the sealed roll, `KS.waxSeal`, at last); when it closes the sky has gone gold and he is asleep. Then **touch him to wake him** (the child is the "one" who came to him).
5. At the top, a side message: "Touch the roll." The tray is empty. Hold to go back down (the game's second "out, then back"); touch the roll under the settle.

**Fear.** *Gentle:* Formalist and Hypocrisy walk off down their ways and are simply not seen again. *Standard:* N: "One went into a great wood, and the other into a wide field full of dark mountains, where he stumbled and fell, and rose no more." (✓ `{104}`)

**People.** Three sleepers (one painted group, they never stand); Formalist and Hypocrisy (stand-ins: Obstinate's and Pliable's walks, retinted: they are also a pair who turn back); Timorous and Mistrust (the same pair running, mirrored: honest reuse, they too are "neighbours who go home"). New: Christian's clamber and his sleep (one clip each, free sources first).

**Sound.** No hymn. The walking theme begins here, hummed, slowing as the hill steepens and stopping in the arbour. Wind, a lark, the spring. When the roll is found: one remembered phrase of Amazing Grace, alone.

**Easter eggs.** Touch the ants on the arbour's step. From the arbour, far below, Level 5's cross tiny on its rise, and further, a thread of smoke from Level 3's hill.

**Tricky points.** The roll is "the assurance of his life and acceptance at the desired haven" (`{109}`): it is assurance, not salvation, that he loses; say nothing that suggests he is lost without it. His sleep is "sinful sleep" in the book; for children "foolish" (Bunyan's own word, `{107}`) is enough.

**Size.** Medium: one tall scenery, two clips, about 16 lines, 6 voices. About $3.

## Level 7. The lions and the Palace Beautiful (`{110}` to `{140}`)

**The picture.** After **Rembrandt** (agreed for Palace Beautiful) by way of **Georges de La Tour**: the supper, one candle among five faces in a great dark, then its answer, the window of the chamber Peace filling with the first sunrise. To stay in the game's own language (the owner is moving back to code-drawn rooms), draw the Palace as the Interpreter's house was last drawn: a **cutaway seen from outside by night** (`houseCut()`), a stately dark house on the hill with rooms lit one at a time: gate lodge, hall and supper, the chamber above with its east window, study, armoury, the roof. One light at a time; the lions outside are two great shapes in the lodge lantern's reach.

**The child's acts.**
1. Hold to walk between the lions "in the midst of the path"; as he passes, the lantern catches the chains. (The awe: they are enormous; the relief: the chains were there all along.)
2. Touch the bell (the porter "rang a bell", `{113}`).
3. Touch the window in the chamber Peace: it opens on the sunrise.
4. In the armoury, touch the pieces one by one: sword, shield, helmet, breastplate, shoes; and the one that is not a thing, **All-prayer** (a soft light that goes into the tray): the child will need it in the Valley.
5. On the roof, touch the far mountains (as the light and the hill were touched): the far light shows above them.

**Fear.** *Gentle:* the lions are dark shapes whose eyes catch the light; they roar once, low. *Standard:* they spring to the end of their chains as he passes.

**People.** Watchful (Good-will's standing picture can stand in); the four damsels (one painted sheet; the Level 1 wife and the parlour's girl as stand-ins); lions (one painted pair, breathing in code). Christian armed: see the tricky point.

**Sound.** The roar; the bell; at supper a quiet consort (viol-like bowed tones, the Slough's `bowed`, in a major key); birds at the window. The walking theme is first heard whole here, sung low at the table.

**Easter eggs.** In the armoury the relics of `{136}` answer a touch, each with one line: "Moses' rod", "the sling and stone with which David slew Goliath", "the trumpets and lamps of Gideon". In the study a book lies open: touch it for one line of `{133}`, "out of weakness were made strong".

**Tricky points.** (1) **Does he wear armour from here on?** Bunyan never takes it off, but uses it only against Apollyon and has the sword "drawn in his hand" through the Valley (`{154}`, `{158}`). Proposal: helmet, shield and sword show in Levels 8 and 9 only; after the Valley they lie in the tray. (2) He stays several days (`{132}`, `{135}`, `{137}`): tell it as one night and one morning, and list it as a departure. (3) "Since he did die on the cross" (`{129}`): the Lord of the hill is named Jesus only in Christian's waking verse; keep that verse.

**Size.** Large in people (five voices, four figures), small in walking. About $4.

## Level 8. Apollyon, in the Valley of Humiliation (`{140}` to `{154}`)

**Beats.** Down the hill "very warily; yet he caught a slip or two" (`{140}`) → bread, a bottle of wine, raisins (`{141}`) → Apollyon comes over the field; "he had no armour for his back", so he stands (`{141}`) → the monster (`{142}`) → "thou art one of my subjects" (`{143}`-`{148}`) → rage; he "straddled quite over the whole breadth of the way" (`{149}`) → the dart, the shield, darts "as thick as hail", wounded in head, hand and foot; "above half a day" (`{150}`) → the fall; the sword flies from his hand; he reaches and catches it: "when I fall I shall arise" (`{151}`) → Apollyon spreads his wings and is gone → thanks (`{153}`) → a hand with leaves of the tree of life; he eats and drinks; on, sword drawn (`{154}`).

**Cut:** the long bargaining (`{144}`-`{148}`) to four exchanges. **Must stay:** "no armour for his back"; whose subject he is; the straddled way; the fallen sword and the two sayings; the hand with the leaves.

- A: "Thou art one of my subjects. How is it, then, that thou hast run away from thy king?" (✓)
- C: "I have given myself to another, even to the King of princes." (`{144}`; check)

- C: "Apollyon, beware what you do; for I am in the King's highway." (✓)

- N: "Apollyon gave him a dreadful fall; and with that Christian's sword flew out of his hand." (✓ `{151}`)

- N: "Then there came to him a hand, with some of the leaves of the tree of life." (✓ `{154}`)

**The picture.** After **William Blake's *Great Red Dragon*** for the wings and **John Martin** for the scale: a wide, quiet, green valley at noon, the gentlest land since the wheat field; then a shadow crosses it. Apollyon is first a darkness over the sun, then a shape whose spread wings run out of both sides of the picture (the game's rule: a vast thing is shown by what does not fit), astride the whole breadth of the way, a red glow in his belly the one light. He must **awe first**: a long still moment with the two figures, one tiny, before a word is said. After, the green comes back, and it is afternoon.

**The child's acts.**
1. Hold to go down; twice his foot slips (a hop of the picture, nothing lost).
2. **Hold up the shield:** hold the shield in the tray (or him) as the first dart comes; it bursts on it in sparks.
3. **Reach for the sword:** it lies on the ground, glinting; the picture is nearly dark; the child touches it. This is the level's one true act, and it enacts "when I fall I shall arise".
4. Touch the leaves in the hand: the red goes out of the picture.
5. Touch the loaf: he sits and eats (the Palace's gift, `{141}`).

**Fear.** *Gentle:* Apollyon is shadow and wings only, no face; the darts are streaks of fire that fall round him; wounds are told as "he grew weaker and weaker"; "prepare thyself to die" becomes "Thou shalt go no further"; the fight is three beats (dart, fall, sword), under half a minute. *Standard:* Bunyan's creature in silhouette (scales, a dragon's wings, fire and smoke from his belly, `{142}`), "wounded him in his head, his hand, and foot", "here will I spill thy soul", and the roaring of `{152}`.

**People.** Apollyon is drawn in code (wings as the Level 3 smoke and cloud are drawn), so no picture is needed; one new voice, deep and slow. Christian armed; a fall and a reach (the kneel clip and Level 2's reach can stand in).

**Sound.** The valley's larks stop before he is seen. A low drone (Level 2's `drum()`), wing-beats as slow gusts. On the sword: a single clear note. No tune until the leaves; then the walking theme, quietly.

**Easter eggs.** Before the shadow comes, the valley answers touches (lark, lily, sheep bell): Part Two calls this valley the most fruitful ground. After, the dropped darts lie in the grass and go out with a hiss when touched.

**Tricky points.** "Blessed Michael helped me" (`{153}`): his thanks are to God; keep "I will give thanks to him that delivered me". Bunyan says the dreadfullest sight was the fight itself, and that Christian smiled only when he had wounded Apollyon: do not make it a romp. "For a season" (`{151}`): Apollyon is not killed.

**Size.** Small in assets, careful in tuning. About $2.

## Level 15. The Flatterer and the Enchanted Ground (`{307}` to `{381}`; Levels 15 and 16 of the old table, joined)

**Beats.** Ignorance, "a very brisk lad", from the country of Conceit by a crooked lane (`{307}`-`{310}`) → a man bound with seven cords, carried back to the door in the hill (`{311}`) → the tale of Little-faith (`{311}`-`{327}`) → two ways, both seeming straight; a man in "a very light robe" says "Follow me"; the way turns by degrees; the net; the white robe falls off (`{328}`) → "did not the Shepherds bid us beware?"; a Shining One with a whip of small cord rends the net; "did you pluck out and read your note?" **Cut:** Turn-away (gentle telling), Little-faith and the whole of Great-grace, all but six lines of Hopeful's story, all but four of Ignorance, all of `{368}`-`{381}`. **Must stay:** Ignorance's crooked lane and "as other people do"; the net and the unread note; Atheist's laugh and Hopeful's answer; the drowsiness and the talk that cures it; the heart of Hopeful's story; Ignorance left behind (he must be remembered at the gate).

**Key lines.**
- C: "How do you think to get in at the gate?" / Ignorance: "As other people do." (✓ `{308}`)
- I: "I know my Lord's will, and I have been a good liver. I pray, fast, pay tithes, and give alms." (✓)
- C: "But you came not in at the wicket-gate. You came in through that crooked lane." (✓ `{309}`)
- The man: "Follow me. It is thither that I am going." (✓ `{328}`)
- N: "He led them both within the compass of a net; and with that the white robe fell off his back." (✓)
- C: "Did not the Shepherds bid us beware of the flatterers?" (✓ `{329}`)
- Shining One: "Did you pluck out and read your note?" / "No." / "Why?" / "We forgot." (✓)
- Shining One: "As many as I love, I rebuke and chasten." (✓ `{330}`)
- Atheist: "I laugh to see what ignorant persons you are. There is no such place as you dream of in all this world." (✓ `{332}`)
- H: "What! No Mount Zion? Did we not see, from the Delectable Mountains, the gate of the city?" (✓ `{334}`)
- H: "I begin to grow so drowsy that I can scarcely hold up mine eyes. Let us lie down here, and take one nap." / C: "By no means, lest sleeping, we never awake more." (✓ `{336}`)

- I: "My heart tells me so." … "You go so fast, I cannot keep pace with you. Do you go on before." (✓ `{356}`, `{367}`)

**The picture.** After **Corot's silver-grey mornings** (*Souvenir de Mortefontaine*): the softest picture in the game, everything a little out of focus, warm still air, poppy-coloured haze low on the ground, the far light blurred. Its beauty is the danger: the land wants them to stay. As Hopeful tells his story the haze clears from the top down, and by the end the far light is sharp again and close. The Flatterer is the one bright figure in the first half: white, shining, brighter than the far light itself (a second, false light, which the game's rule "one light" forbids, so it looks wrong before the child knows why).

**The child's acts.**
1. At the fork the tray's note glints. The storyteller keeps the book: they do not read it, and follow. (No act here: the missing act is the point.)
2. In the net, touch the Shining One's hand; the net tears like cobweb. **Then touch the note:** it opens, too late, and shows the straight way.
3. Touch Atheist: he only laughs. Touch the far light: Hopeful's answer.
4. **Keep them awake.** The picture's edges darken and soften like closing eyes, by themselves, slowly (the words of the level are what move; say so in the notes as the owner's kind of exception). Each touch on Hopeful brings the next line of his story and a thought in the sky, and the edges open. If the child does nothing, nothing bad happens: they only stand and nod, and the side message comes again.
5. Hold to walk on; Ignorance, on a further level, falls slowly behind and out of the picture.

**Fear.** *Gentle:* Turn-away is cut; the Shining One "led them back, and bid them take heed"; no whip. *Standard:* "he chastised them sore, to teach them the good way" (✓ `{330}`) in one line, not shown; Turn-away seen far off in a dark lane, carried toward the door in the hill (which prepares the last scene).

**People.** Ignorance (new: a brisk lad; a stand-in could be Pliable's walk, retinted, but he must not be mistaken for Pliable: better his own), the Flatterer (see below), a Shining One (light, as at the Cross), Atheist (Obstinate's walk mirrored: another mocker walking the wrong way).

**Sound.** A lulling tune that never resolves, slowing; Hopeful's story brings the walking theme in under it and takes over. Bees. Atheist's laugh, the only loud thing.

**Easter eggs.** In the haze, arbours and soft benches by the road (Part Two's Enchanted Ground has them): touch one and Christian shakes his head. Touch a poppy: it nods shut.

**Tricky points.** (1) **The Flatterer is "a man black of flesh, but covered with a very light robe"** (`{328}`). Bunyan means a false "angel of light" (2 Cor. 11:14, his own margin), but the words must not be used as written. In this game every figure is a dark silhouette anyway: tell it as "a man in a white shining robe", and when the robe falls he is "a shadow". List the departure and tell the owner. (2) Ignorance is not a villain: he is pleasant, sure of himself, and wrong about one thing (he trusts his own good heart). The game should make the child like him, or the ending means nothing. (3) Hopeful's story is the plainest statement of the gospel in the second half; it belongs to the level's one playable act.

**Size.** Medium, heavy in words (must be cut hardest of all). About $3.

## Level 16. Beulah, the River, and the Celestial City (`{382}` to `{403}`)

**Beats.** The country of Beulah: birds, flowers, "the sun shineth night and day"; within sight of the City; Shining Ones walk here (`{382}`) → the City of pearl and gold; "Christian with desire fell sick" (`{383}`) → orchards, vineyards, the King's gardener; they sleep and talk in their sleep (`{384}`, `{385}`) → the City too bright to look on but "through an instrument made for that purpose" (`{386}`) → two men in raiment that shone like gold: "You have but two difficulties more" (`{386}`-`{388}`) → the River: no bridge, very deep; "deeper or shallower as you believe in the King of the place" (`{389}`, `{390}`) → Christian sinks; a great darkness; Hopeful keeps his head above water; "I feel the bottom, and it is good"; "It is you they wait for" (`{391}`, `{392}`) → "Oh, I see him again!"; ground to stand on; over (`{393}`) → up the hill with ease, their mortal garments left in the river (`{394}`) → what awaits them (`{395}`) → the heavenly host; the King's trumpeters; all the bells of the City (`{396}`, `{397}`) → the gate and its writing; they give in their certificates; "Where are the men?"; the gate opened (`{398}`, `{399}`) → transfigured; harps and crowns; "ENTER YE INTO THE JOY OF YOUR LORD" (`{400}`) → the dreamer looks in; "I wished myself among them" (`{401}`, `{402}`) → Ignorance (`{403}`) → "So I awoke, and behold it was a dream."

**Cut:** the Shining Ones' long promise (`{395}`) to two lines; the garden to a touch. **Must stay:** everything from the river's edge to "I wished myself among them", nearly whole. This is the one level allowed to be long (as Level 4 was), about 4 minutes.

**Key lines.**

- H: "Be of good cheer; Jesus Christ maketh thee whole." / C: "Oh, I see him again! And he tells me: When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee." (✓ `{393}`)

**The picture.** After **Turner** (agreed for the end of the game): light dissolving everything. Three pictures that are one, each with less in it than the last. *Beulah:* every colour the game has withheld, for the first time uncapped (`GOLD_MAX` 1), birds, orchards, the City a brightness on a hill over water. *The River:* Level 2's composition come back, a wide grey water under a great sky, two small figures wading; but the far bank is light, not an oak. In the middle it goes dark as the Slough's fears went dark (`darkMist`), and for a few seconds the game is back at its beginning. *The City:* no buildings drawn. A hill of light, a gate that is only two dark uprights against it, and when it opens the screen goes slowly to white-gold: the far light, which has been a small disc on the horizon since the first screen of Level 1, fills the whole picture. The child has been walking toward that dot for sixteen levels and now it is everything.

**The child's acts.**
1. Beulah answers every touch (birds, blossom, fruit, the gardener's gate): the one place in the game that is all easter egg. Then the glass again: now it does not shake.
2. **Hold to wade** (Level 2's wading, the same clip, the same act: deliberately). Holding moves him less and less; the dark comes down.
3. In the dark, a side message, the simplest in the game: "Touch your friend." Hopeful's hand. "I feel the bottom, and it is good." Third phrase of Amazing Grace, alone.
4. Hold again: the ground comes up under him; they walk out in light, the grey clothes left on the water.
5. **The trumpets and the bells.** The King's trumpeters stand along the way up: each, touched, sounds one note, and the notes are the hymn (Level 1's lights, one note each, `PHRASES`; here the child plays it, whole, at the pace they choose). The bells of the City answer each phrase by themselves.
6. **Give in the roll.** At the gate the roll in the tray glows. The child touches it and it rises to the hands above the gate. A long silence. "Where are the men?" The gate opens. No act after this: the child only watches.

**How to make it the most moving moment of the game.**
- **Everything returns.** The River is the Slough (same act, same clip, same dark); the hand is Help's hand; the trumpets are Level 1's lights; the bells are Level 1's bells; the roll is the Cross's; the trumpet is Faithful's. Nothing new is needed because everything has been prepared.
- **Restraint before release.** The darkest ten seconds since Level 2 come ninety seconds before the end. Then stillness: after the roll goes up, hold the silence longer than is comfortable before the King speaks.
- **Faithful at the gate.** `{395}` promises "you shall enjoy your friends again that are gone thither before you". Among the figures of light who come out, one stands a little forward, in Faithful's outline. No line, no name. (A departure by showing; warranted by the promise. Owner's choice.)
- **The dreamer's wish.** "I wished myself among them" should be said after the gates have shut and the picture has come back down from white to the quiet river bank, empty. It is the truest line in the book for a child holding a phone.
- **Close the book.** The game began by opening a book. After "So I awoke, and behold it was a dream", the scene draws back onto the page, the leaf turns, the cover closes (`coverArt()`: the same cover, the tiny pilgrim gone from the hill in its window, only the far light there). Under it, in the quiet italic of the title page, two lines of Bunyan's Conclusion (`{404}`): "Put by the curtains, look within my veil, / Turn up my metaphors, and do not fail."
- **His family.** Level 1's lit door and Level 7's tears are never answered in Part One. Bunyan answered them in Part Two (his wife and the four boys follow him). One quiet line on the last contents page, as a line of the book and not of the game: "Now it came to pass that his wife, and their four boys, went after him. But that is another dream." A departure (it is outside Part One), true to Bunyan, and the kindest thing the game can tell a child who has worried about that door since the first screen. Owner's choice.

**Ignorance (`{403}`).** In the book this comes after the gates shut and is the last event before waking: he is ferried over by Vain-hope, comes up alone, "fumbled in his bosom for [a certificate], and found none", "answered never a word", and the two Shining Ones bind him and carry him "to the door that I saw in the side of the hill ... Then I saw that there was a way to hell, even from the gates of heaven". Three ways to handle it:
- **A. Gentle, in the book's place (recommended).** Keep the order and the quiet. A boat on the river with a second figure poling it (so the child sees that he did not wade); he climbs alone; no one meets him; he knocks. "They asked him for his certificate. So he fumbled in his bosom for one, and found none." "And the man answered never a word." The gate stays shut. He is led away, small, down the far side toward the door in the hill the Shepherds showed (seen, not entered). N: "Then I saw that there was a way down, even from the gates of heaven." Then at once the waking. No binding, no word "hell", no judgment spoken. The child's last act of the game has been to give in the roll; here is one with none: nothing needs explaining.
- **B. Standard** (with "Shew the darker places" on): Bunyan's words whole.
- **C. Moved or left out.** Tell it before the gates (so the game ends on glory), or leave it out and say only, earlier, "Ignorance came after". I advise against both: Bunyan chose to end on a warning, the owner's rule is the book's order, and A is gentle enough. But this is a ruling for the owner.
- In all three: the fault is not that he was simple or unlearned (the name misleads a modern child) but that he trusted his own goodness and would have no certificate. The Level 15 lines ("As other people do"; "My heart tells me so") are what make this fair.

**Fear.** The River is death, and Bunyan calls it so in his heading (`{392}`). *Gentle:* say only what the book's narrative says (a river, deep, no bridge); the darkness is short; Hopeful never lets go; "the sorrows of death" is not spoken. *Standard:* "the sorrows of death have compassed me about" and "he was troubled with apparitions" as shapes in the dark (the Valley's). Either way the child should not be told in so many words what the River is; those who are ready will know.

**People.** Two Shining Ones (light, as at the Cross), the gardener (a Shepherd's figure), trumpeters and the host (figures of light, code), the watchers over the gate (dark heads against the light), Ignorance and the ferryman. Christian and Hopeful wading (Level 2's `christian-wade` and `christian-stuck` clips serve for Christian if the burden is painted out; Hopeful needs one wading clip); both as figures of light at the end (their pictures drawn with 'lighter', as the Shining Ones are).

**Size.** The largest. Perhaps $6 to $8, most of it voices and Hopeful's clip; the City itself is nearly free, because it is light.
