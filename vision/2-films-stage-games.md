# How *The Pilgrim's Progress* has been adapted, and what the game can borrow

Research for the children's story game (Part One, 17 levels). Written 21 Nov 2026.

## How far to trust this

- **Checked by web search in this session** (marked ✔): the facts carry a source link.
- **From my own knowledge, not re-checked** (marked ◇): most reference sites (Wikipedia, TV Tropes, Common Sense Media, The Crankie Factory) could not be opened from this session, only searched. Treat ◇ items as leads to confirm before quoting them to anyone. To open them, those hosts would have to be allowed in the session's network settings.
- I could not watch or listen to anything; judgments of tone come from reviews.

## 1. Films and television

### 1.1 The 1912 silent film ✔ (thin sources)
- Released Oct 1912 by Hochstetter Utility Co.; trade listings describe it in two parts: **a life of Bunyan (2 reels) and then the allegory (3 reels)**; some listings call it lost. [filmweb](https://www.filmweb.pl/film/Pilgrim%27s+Progress-1912-649229), [Media History Digital Library](https://lantern.mediahist.org/catalog/motionpicturenew82unse_0355), [MUBI](https://mubi.com/en/us/films/pilgrim-s-progress-1912)
- **Idea:** the very first screen version framed the dream with the dreamer. The opera (2.1), the BBC radio play and the 1978 film do the same. Bunyan's first sentence is already on the game's contents page; the dreamer could return once, at the very end ("So I awoke, and behold it was a dream").

### 1.2 The 1950 Baptista cartoon ✔
- Baptista Film Mission, 1950: "probably the first feature-length evangelical cartoon"; a 35-minute cut was issued in 1978. [Wheaton archives](https://fromthevault.wheaton.edu/2021/11/01/little-hollywood-on-the-prairie/), [Place for Truth review](https://placefortruth.org/node/36905)
- Limited animation: mostly held paintings with small moves and a narrator. That is the same economy the game uses (breathing stills, a few real clips).

### 1.3 Ken Anderson's 1978 live-action film (and *Christiana*, 1979) ✔
- Shot in Ireland; narrated by a well-known gospel singer; Liam Neeson's first film. [IMDb](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0359836), [Wikipedia: Ken Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Anderson_(filmmaker))
- **The standout trick: doubling.** One actor (Neeson) is credited as **Evangelist, Good-will, the Interpreter and Christ**; one actor (Maurice O'Callaghan) as **Apollyon, Obstinate, Worldly Wiseman and the Prince (of Vanity)**. [Plex cast list](https://watch.plex.tv/movie/pilgrims-progress), [TMDB](https://themoviedb.org/movie/67897). Sources disagree on a few roles.
- Criticisms: "incomplete, leaving out many details", flat, "fails to convey emotion or intensity"; IMDb about 5.3. [Atom reviews](https://www.atomtickets.com/movies/pilgrims-progress/277616/reviews)
- **Idea for the game:** a quiet family likeness. The helpers (Evangelist, Help, Good-will, the Interpreter, the Shepherds, the Shining Ones) could share one gesture or one musical phrase; the tempters (Wiseman, Apollyon's voice, By-ends, Demas, the Flatterer) could share the too-sweet tune Level 3 already has (`sweet()`). A child feels "I know that tune, be careful" before a word is said. It also saves money: one tempter's walk re-clothed.

### 1.4 *Dangerous Journey* (Yorkshire Television, 1985) ✔
- **Not animation: the book's still watercolours (Alan Parry) with a dramatic narrator (Paul Copley), the camera moving over them.** Nine parts of about 15 minutes, each with a recap and a "next time". Recommended "ages six and older"; "highly recommended for religious education". [Video Librarian](https://videolibrarian.com/reviews/tv/dangerous-journey/), [Christian Answers review](https://christiananswers.net/spotlight/movies/pre2000/rvu-dj.html), [Christian History Institute](https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/store/dvd/500777d/dangerous-journey)
- ◇ The pictures are remembered as grotesque and frightening in places (Apollyon scaled and winged, Giant Despair huge and hairy, the mouth of hell). It keeps Faithful's death and Giant Despair; it uses Bunyan's words heavily abridged.
- **Why it matters most of all the films:** it is the nearest thing to this game: painted stills, one voice, short parts, a child audience. Its lessons: (1) **nine 15-minute parts worked for children**, so 17 short levels is a sound shape; (2) a recap in one sentence at the start of each part; (3) a single good voice carries it; (4) the fright came from *detail* in the monsters. The game's rule of silhouettes and "awe before fright" is the safer road.

### 1.5 The 2019 computer-animated film (Revelation Media) ✔
- Written and directed by Robert Fernandez; voices include John Rhys-Davies; released free to missions. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pilgrim%27s_Progress_(film)), [Variety](https://variety.com/2019/film/reviews/the-pilgrims-progress-review-1203191406)
- Reviews: the Guardian, "tiring trudge to the Celestial City", "plodding, sanctimonious"; another, the look "resembles an aircraft safety video"; Film Stories, "some cracking, genuinely nightmare-inducing demons"; Variety, a "reminder of faith's rewards" that will not win new converts. [Guardian via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-guardian-usa/20191024/282063393750602), [Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/reviews/the-pilgrims-progress-review/), [Rotten Tomatoes](https://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/the_pilgrims_progress_2019/reviews), [Church Times](https://churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2019/25-october/books-arts/film/film-review-the-pilgrim-s-progress-and-corpus-christi)
- Parents' guides: Common Sense 8+; the Australian guide says not for under 8 and guidance to 13 "due to violence, scary scenes and themes"; "not a movie for young children despite being animated". [Common Sense Media](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/movie-reviews/the-pilgrims-progress), [Children and Media Australia](https://childrenandmedia.org.au/movie-reviews/movies/the-pilgrims-progress), [Raising Children](https://raisingchildren.net.au/guides/movie-reviews/the-pilgrims-progress), [Plugged In](https://www.pluggedin.com/?p=3466)
- ◇ It gives Apollyon a throne-room framing as ruler of the City of Destruction, shows the fight as a monster battle, shows Faithful's execution at Vanity Fair, a huge Giant Despair with a club, and Christian sinking in the River; it trims Talkative, By-ends and Ignorance.
- **Lessons:** the two complaints are **trudge** (walk, talk, walk, talk at one pace for two hours) and **demons too real for the age group**. The game's answers already exist: under two minutes of speech a level, an act every five lines, and darkness shown as darkness. The film proves the risk of rendering monsters literally.

- **2005 animation and 2011 game by the later maker of *Five Nights at Freddy's*** ✔: an eight-part computer animation (DVD about 2005, YouTube 2007), then a **JRPG-style PC game (2011, Hope Animation)** in which Christian fights Beelzebub, Apollyon, Shame and Giant Despair in turn-based battles; the maker said he kept it as faithful as he could. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim%27s_Progress:_Journey_to_Heaven), [Dove](https://dove.org/review/7484-pilgrims-progress-journey-to-heaven/), [CBN](https://www2.cbn.com/entertainment/screen/ElliottB_PilgrimsProgress.aspx). Relevant to the owner's idea of a year-2200 look: modern dress has been done and was accepted.

- ***Little Pilgrim's Progress*** ✔: no animated film was found. ## 2. Stage

### 2.1 Vaughan Williams, *The Pilgrim's Progress* (Covent Garden, 1951): "a Morality"
Checked ✔: premiere 26 April 1951; he called it a Morality, not an opera; it opens on the psalm tune **"York"** with Bunyan in Bedford Gaol finishing his book; a 1951 reviewer noted the libretto "never shows Pilgrim faltering": **no Slough of Despond, no Doubting Castle**, and the Interpreter's House merged with House Beautiful; the three Shining Ones are **heard from offstage** as the burden goes; the Interpreter marks his forehead and he is given a white robe; a **Nocturne** in which Watchful the porter prays for the sleeping house; Act II opens "This is the King's Highway" (the Arming); the composer called Apollyon and Vanity Fair the "exciting" scenes needed to offset the mystical mood and worried Vanity Fair was over before audiences grasped it; he later called the first production a flop. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pilgrim%27s_Progress_(opera)), [Spectator, 4 May 1951](https://archive.spectator.co.uk/article/4th-may-1951/13/music), [RVW letters](https://vaughanwilliamsfoundation.org/letter/vwl2226), [BBC Music Magazine feature](https://www.classical-music.com/features/articles/pilgrims-progress-opera-vaughan-williams/), [Gramophone](https://gramophone.co.uk/review/vaughan-williams-the-pilgrims-progress-0), [Times Higher Education on the ENO revival](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/node/111348)

From knowledge ◇:
- The hero is "Pilgrim", not Christian. **Faithful and Hopeful are cut**: Pilgrim alone is tried at Vanity Fair.
- **Apollyon is a voice more than a body**: a bass chanting on almost one note, offstage and amplified, with a vast shape or shadow; the "Doleful Creatures" are a chorus. After the fight two heavenly beings heal him with **leaves from the Tree of Life and the Water of Life** (this is Bunyan's: "a hand, with some of the leaves of the tree of life").
- The **key called Promise** is moved from Doubting Castle to the prison at Vanity Fair: Pilgrim despairs alone, remembers the key in his bosom, and the door opens on moonlight and the open road.
- Act IV begins with a **Woodcutter's Boy** singing "He that is down need fear no fall" (Bunyan's shepherd boy's song from Part Two), then the comic Mister and Madam By-Ends.
- ***The Shepherds of the Delectable Mountains*** (1922, one act, folded into Act IV): evening, three shepherds, **a bird sings the 23rd Psalm**, a Celestial Messenger pierces Pilgrim's heart with an arrow as his summons; the crossing is done with **offstage trumpets and Alleluias across the water** rather than shown.
- The epilogue returns to "York" and Bunyan offering his book. The same themes run through his Fifth Symphony.
- He also wrote the music for a **1942 to 1943 BBC radio dramatization** and for a 1906 amateur staging; the 1954 Cambridge student production was the staging he felt got it right.

**What the game can take:** (1) one plain old psalm or hymn tune as a frame, opening and closing; (2) helpers heard before they are seen; (3) Apollyon as *sound and shadow*; (4) healing leaves after the fight as the child's act; (5) the key remembered; (6) a child's voice singing as a palate cleanser after a dark level; (7) the bird that sings a psalm; (8) the River ended by sound from the far side; (9) the warning in the criticism: without Pilgrim *faltering* it was thought static. The game keeps the Slough and Doubting Castle, and should keep him weak in them.

### 2.2 Moving panoramas (1850 to 1851) ✔
- "The Grand Moving Panorama of Pilgrim's Progress": a painted canvas about **800 feet long**, wound from one spool to another past a frame, **with a narrator and music, about two hours**; opened in New York in 1850 and ran six months; scene designs credited to leading landscape painters of the day (the Land of Beulah design is attributed to Jasper Cropsey; others to Frederic Church, Henry Courtney Selous, Joseph Kyle, Edward May, Jacob Dallas); a second version survives at the Saco Museum, Maine, restored and performed again with live narration and music from 2012. [Press Herald](https://www.pressherald.com/?p=3788774), [Conservators Converse / NYT report](https://resources.culturalheritage.org/conservators-converse/?p=5021), [ArtsJournal](https://www.artsjournal.com/realcleararts/2012/07/sacos-panorama-also-comes-to-light.html), [The Crankie Factory](https://thecrankiefactory.com/115034656.html), [Victorian Web: Selous](https://victorianweb.org/art/illustration/selous/13.html), [Hyperallergic on panoramas](https://hyperallergic.com/280532/researchers-unroll-a-rare-19th-century-painted-panorama/)
- ◇ The Valley of the Shadow design is usually credited to Church; panoramas of the time lit some scenes from behind (transparencies) so fires, moons and the City glowed through the cloth.
- **This is the game's direct ancestor**: a side-scrolling painted Pilgrim's Progress by landscape masters, with one storyteller and music. Borrow: (1) **light from behind** for the special thing in each level (the game already does this with the far light, the door at the gate, the sun behind the cross); (2) a **panorama mode** as a reward after finishing: the whole journey scrolled as one long ribbon, the child dragging it, each level's "one great picture" passing in turn with a line of narration; (3) a master painter per scene, which the owner already does.

### 2.3 Magic-lantern sets ✔
- Many British sets (12 to 60 slides: Theobald, Riley Brothers 1895, Newton and others), each sold with a printed **"reading"**, and some as a **"service of song"** or "lantern service": slide, short reading, then everyone sings a hymn. Sunday School Union editions supplied the pictures. [Lucerna database, Exeter](https://lucerna.exeter.ac.uk/set/index.php?id=3005883), [Riley Brothers set](https://lucerna.exeter.ac.uk/set/index.php?id=3001072), [Lantern services](https://lucerna.exeter.ac.uk/text/index.php?id=4009137), [Theobald catalogue](https://lantern.mediahist.org/catalog/TheobaldCatalog_0221)
- ◇ Lanternists used **dissolving views** (one slide melting into another: day into night, the gate shut into the gate open) and slipping slides for one small movement.
- Borrow: (1) the **12-slide discipline**: the whole book told in a dozen pictures and a few sentences each; a test for every level, "what is this level's one slide and its three sentences?"; (2) the **dissolve** as the game's main transition (already used for Passion's rags and the veil of mist); (3) picture, a few words, then *music the audience makes*: the child's notes along the road are the service of song.

### 2.4 Theatre for families and puppets
- A 2024 touring stage version (Logos Theatre, shown at the Museum of the Bible) ✔: the Slough of Despond staged with **gauzy hangings like Spanish moss** and light; the review calls the **villains "perhaps the most skillfully devised aspect"**. [DC Theater Arts](https://dctheaterarts.org/2024/09/01/a-stirring-heros-journey-in-pilgrims-progress-at-museum-of-the-bible/). ◇ That company is known for very large puppets worked by several people in view.
- A fringe version, "A Progress" ✔: "resourceful and witty", "gleefully childish". [Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/london/theatre/the-pilgrims-progress). An Edinburgh church staging ✔: [British Theatre Guide](https://www.britishtheatreguide.info/reviews/the-pilgrim-s-p-edinburgh-elim-11973).
- I found no reviewed puppet-only production. From the general practice of puppet and shadow theatre ◇: the burden is a real sack that visibly weighs on the actor and is physically dropped; giants are shadows thrown large on a cloth; mud and water are blue and brown silk shaken by hands.
- Borrow: **shadow play for the giants and fiends** (a thing thrown large on a wall is awe, not gore: Level 3's gate already throws Christian's shadow on the wall); cloth and mist for the Slough and the River.

The Valley of the Shadow is by Bunyan's own telling a level of sounds: "doleful voices and rushings to and fro", a whispering voice he takes for his own, and then **another pilgrim's voice ahead in the dark** saying the 23rd Psalm. Audio versions make that voice the turning point. The game makes sound in the browser already (drums in the Slough, the pad in Level 3): Level 9 can be its radio play.

## 4. Board and parlour games

- **The New Game of Human Life** (London, 1790) ◇: a spiral of 84 squares, one per year of life, played with a teetotum (a spinner, because dice were thought improper for children); virtues send you forward, vices back.
- **The Mansion of Happiness** (England about 1800; USA 1843) ✔: a spiral track of virtues and vices ending in the centre. [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/mansion-of-happiness-board-game-history/)
- **Games of the Pilgrim's Progress, Going to Sunday School and Tower of Babel** (McLoughlin Brothers, 1875) ✔: the track runs from the City of Destruction past the Cross and the Valley; **each player moves two pieces, a Pilgrim and a Staff**, until the Pilgrim reaches Giant Despair; then the Pilgrim alone must land **exactly on the Golden Gate**. [Analog Games](https://www.analog.games//gameItem/show/419cd83f965e201d01965f5a5e2862b2?lang=EN), [Google Arts & Culture / The Strong](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/board-game-game-of-pilgrim-s-progress-going-to-sunday-school-tower-of-babel-mcloughlin-brothers/pAFFAHEGcGcXmA)
- ***Little Women*** (1868) ◇: chapter 1 is "Playing Pilgrims": as little girls they tied piece-bags on their backs for burdens, took hats, sticks and rolls of paper, and travelled **from the cellar (the City of Destruction) up through the house to the roof (the Celestial City)**, where they had "all the lovely things". Chapters are named for the stations ("Burdens", "Beth Finds the Palace Beautiful", "Amy's Valley of Humiliation", "Jo Meets Apollyon", "Meg Goes to Vanity Fair"), and each girl finds a small guide-book under her pillow.
- **Borrow:** (1) **the map**. Every one of these is a path on a board; children love to see where they are. The contents page could open into a hand-drawn road-map of the whole journey in the 1678 engraving style, places inked in as they are reached, the pointing hand where the child stands (no score, only place). (2) **Pilgrim and Staff**: two things that travel together and then one goes on alone. In the book that is exactly Faithful, then Hopeful, then the River. (3) Alcott's game is **vertical**: cellar to roof. The game's levels could, taken together, slowly climb: low and wet at the start (city, slough), high and clear at the end (mountains, Beulah). (4) The roll, the book, the key and the coat as **things carried**, already begun in the tray.

## 5. Video games

- **2011 PC game** ✔ (see 1.6): turn-based battles against the book's enemies. **2024 Steam "The Pilgrim's Progress", Chapter 1** ✔: an action adventure in which "hitting an enemy raises your faith, getting hit lowers it"; one user review at the time of the search. - I found no notable App Store, Android or VR adaptation, and no critic-reviewed game of the book.
- **Finding:** every game of the book so far turns it into **combat with a faith meter**. Nobody has made the gentle walking story. Bunyan's own fight is won by a sword that "flew out of his hand" being caught again and by words, not by hit points; a no-fail version is closer to the text and the field is empty.

## 6. Literary riffs that suggest ideas ◇

- **Hawthorne, "The Celestial Railroad" (1843).** A railway now runs from the City of Destruction: a bridge over the Slough, burdens put in the baggage car, a tunnel through Hill Difficulty whose rubble fills the Valley of Humiliation, gas lamps in the Valley of the Shadow, Apollyon driving the engine, a smooth guide who vanishes laughing at the river; two pilgrims on foot are jeered at from the windows. *Idea:* the easy way always runs **alongside**. In By-path Meadow, at Demas's mine and on the Enchanted Ground the tempting road can be seen beside the true one for a long while, smooth and well lit. Also a strong fit if the year-2200 version is ever made.
- **C.S. Lewis, *The Pilgrim's Regress* (1933).** A map; a far island glimpsed in childhood that pulls the pilgrim on; and a **return journey in which the same country looks different**. *Idea:* after the Cross, or after the whole game, earlier levels can be walked again and be changed: no burden on his back, the Slough's steps visible, the notes in tune. Levels 2 and 3 already bend the tune out of true; a second walk could put it right.
- **Thackeray, *Vanity Fair* (1848).** He frames the novel as a **puppet show** with the author as showman. *Idea:* Vanity Fair as a toy theatre, bright, flat and noisy, the only level with many colours.
- **Enid Blyton, *The Land of Far-Beyond* (1942).** A children's retelling: children with burdens they cannot take off. Proof that the burden, kept literal, is what children hold on to.
- **William Blake's 28 watercolours** (1824 to 1827) and the 1678 to 1680s woodcuts: a sleeping dreamer drawn beside the story. A possible master for the Valley of the Shadow.

## 7. What every adaptation teaches

1. **The burden is the hook.** Every children's version keeps it literal and visible. The game's choice to carry it for four levels and drop it at the Cross is the strongest structural asset it has.
2. **Everyone cuts the talk.** The opera cuts Faithful, Hopeful, the Slough and the Giant; films drop Talkative, By-ends, Ignorance. The usual casualties are Levels 10, 12 and 15 of the plan. They need a strong *picture and act* each or they will be the game's "trudge".
3. **Complaints repeat:** trudge at one pace; preachy; demons too frightening for the age that animation attracts. Praise repeats: the voice, the pictures, the burden falling, the River.
4. **Fear works best as sound, shadow and absence**; the versions that draw monsters in detail get age warnings.
5. **Doubling** gives a child a way to read characters without words.
6. **Framing with the dreamer and one old tune** gives a beginning and an end.
7. **Short parts with a one-line recap** worked for children in 1985.

## 8. Level by level: ideas to borrow

Tags name the source. "(ours)" would be a departure to list.

**1. City of Destruction** (built)
- A single line of the dreamer at the very start exists already; keep the matching last line for Level 17. [1912 film; opera]
- In a later "second walk", the wheat field with no burden on his back. [Lewis]
- The contents page as a road-map that inks in. [board games]

**2. Slough of Despond** (built)
- Cloth-and-light mire is what the stage does; the game's mist and darkness already match. [Logos Theatre]
- Second walk: the steps lit and plain to see. [Lewis; Bunyan `{35}`]
- The opera was faulted for cutting this; it is where he first *falters*. Keep him weak. [Spectator 1951]

**3. Worldly Wiseman and the hill** (built)
- Make Wiseman's sweet tune the **tempters' tune** for the whole game. [1978 doubling]
- The far smooth road to the village, seen beside the true way. [Hawthorne]

**4. Wicket-Gate and the Interpreter's House** (house built)
- The Interpreter's rooms are already a lantern show: each room one slide and its reading. Keep each to a 12-slide economy. [magic lantern]
- The opera's **Nocturne**: a held, quiet prayer over the sleeping house. One still night beat before he leaves would rest the child after seven rooms. [opera] (ours)
- The mark on the forehead and the robe given *in* the house in the opera; the game rightly keeps them for the Cross.

**5. The Cross** (built)
- The opera has the Shining Ones **heard before seen**, from far off; their first words could come from the light before the figures form. [opera]
- After this level unlock the "second walk" of Levels 1 to 3 without the burden. [Lewis] (ours)
- This is where the board games mark the first great station: ink the map in gold here. [McLoughlin 1875]

**6. Simple, Sloth and Presumption; Formalist and Hypocrisy; Hill Difficulty; the lost roll; the lions**
- **The lost roll is the book's own gentle "game" beat**: he sleeps in the arbour, the roll slips from his hand, he climbs on, finds it gone, and must go back. Let the child see it fall (they will want to shout), touch the empty tray later to discover it gone, and walk back down in the dusk to find it glinting under the bench. Nothing can be failed; the cost is the walk, as in Bunyan ("thus he went back, carefully looking on this side and on that"). [Bunyan; Sky/Journey "small loss"]
- Three ways at the hill's foot: Danger and Destruction go round, **smooth and level, in view**; the true way goes straight up. Show the two side paths running off beside. [Hawthorne]
- Formalist and Hypocrisy tumble over the wall rather than come by the gate: comic relief, a quick silhouette gag. [fringe staging "gleefully childish"]
- **The lions as roar and shadow first**: heard round the bend, thrown large on the rock by the porter's lantern, then seen chained as he keeps to the middle of the path. The child's act: hold to walk the middle line while the porter calls. [shadow theatre; opera's offstage Apollyon]
- Vertical feeling: this is the first real climb of the game. [Alcott's cellar to roof]

**7. Palace Beautiful**
- The opera's Watchful and his night prayer; morning in the chamber "whose name was Peace", the window opening toward the sunrise: a single touch opens the shutters and the Delectable Mountains are seen far off (Bunyan shows them from the roof). This plants Level 14. [opera; Bunyan]
- **The Arming** as a tactile sequence in the manner of *Florence*: the child touches helmet, breastplate, shield, sword, shoes, and each settles on him ("All-prayer" last). The opera opens Act II with it as a ceremony, "This is the King's Highway". [opera; Florence]
- The "rarities" shown in the study (Moses' rod, David's sling) as touchable keepsakes seen up close, like the Bible and the roll. [keepsakes; Unpacking]
- Music: the helpers' phrase, sung for the first time by women's voices. [doubling]

**8. Apollyon**
- **Sound and shadow, not a rendered monster.** A voice on nearly one note, low, from offstage; a vast wing-shape across the sky after John Martin; the camera low. The 2019 film and Dangerous Journey drew him in detail and earned age warnings. [opera ◇; Film Stories; Common Sense]
- The fight in three beats from Bunyan, no hit points: darts fall like hail on the shield (the child holds the shield up: hold, as for walking); the sword flies from his hand (silence, dark); **the child touches the sword and he catches it** ("Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall I shall arise"); Apollyon "spread forth his dragon's wings, and sped him away". One act decides it, and it cannot be failed, only waited for. [Bunyan; Journey's no-fail climax]
- **Healing leaves**: afterwards a hand brings leaves of the tree of life; the child lays them on. A quiet, kind end. [opera; Bunyan]
- Music: no tune during the fight, drums only; the helpers' phrase returns with the leaves. Every game so far made this a battle system; do the opposite. [Steam 2024; 2011 game]

**9. Valley of the Shadow of Death**
- **Make this the radio play.** Nearly black; the path a pale thread between the ditch and the quag; the things in it are *sounds* placed left and right. The far light is gone for the first time; the only light is small and close to him. [audio dramas; Inside/Limbo staging]
- Bunyan's own best device: a whisperer at his ear puts wicked words into his head and he thinks they are his own. One whispered line, unvoiced by the narrator, would be chilling enough; or leave it as a breath only. Owner's ruling.
- **The turn: a voice ahead in the dark**, "Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil". He is not alone. Then day breaks ("He hath turned the shadow of death into the morning") and he looks back and sees what he came through. This is the moment audio versions build to. [Bunyan; BBC; opera's Psalm 23 bird]
- The sword is put up and "All-prayer" used instead: the child's act could be simply *holding still* with a finger on him while the noise passes (the only level where stillness is the act). [ours]
- Panorama makers gave this scene to their best landscape painter; treat it as the game's great dark picture (Martin, or Blake). [Saco panorama]

**10. Faithful and Talkative**
- **Company as the reward** after two lonely dark levels: he runs to catch Faithful up, overreaches, falls, and Faithful helps him up (Bunyan's own small comedy). A second walker beside him changes the whole feel, as a companion does in *Journey* and *Sky*. [Journey; McLoughlin's Pilgrim and Staff]
- Faithful tells his road in pictures in the sky as he walks, in the manner of Level 2's "thoughts" (Wanton, Adam the First, Shame): three thought-pictures, not three talks. [Old Man's Journey's memory stills]
- **Talkative as a sound joke**: his words keep coming, the word strip fills, and nothing in the picture changes; the hidden notes fall silent near him. "He is best abroad; near home he is ugly enough." Most films cut him; if kept, keep him under a minute. [2019 film's cuts]

**11. Vanity Fair and Faithful's death**
- The fair as a **toy theatre or puppet show**: the one loud, many-coloured level, flat booths like a paper stage, after the woodblock night scenes the owner listed. Everything can be touched and everything is hollow (touch a jewel, it is paint; touch a crown, it is card). The pilgrims' answer to "What will ye buy?" is "We buy the truth". [Thackeray; opera's Vanity Fair; Unpacking-style touching]
- The opera worried the scene was over before it was understood: give the fair time before the trial. [RVW letters]
- **Faithful's death by Bunyan's own turn of the camera**: he says it in one sentence and at once shows "a chariot and a couple of horses waiting for Faithful", who is "carried up through the clouds, with sound of trumpet, the nearest way to the Celestial Gate". Show the crowd close in as silhouettes, the picture rise above their heads, and the chariot of light go up. No act of cruelty on screen. [Bunyan; opera's offstage endings] Owner's ruling.
- Christian in the cage: the opera's best invention is the prisoner alone remembering he has a key. In the book the key belongs to Level 13; do not spend it here, but the *quiet after the noise* is worth copying. [opera]
- Hopeful steps out of the crowd of onlookers: the Staff replaced. [McLoughlin]

**12. Hopeful, By-ends, Demas's silver mine, Lot's wife**
- By-ends and his friends as the opera's comic pair: fair-weather walkers who stop the moment it rains. A weather gag: the sky clouds and they are simply not there. [opera's Mister and Madam By-Ends]
- **Demas's mine "a little off the road"**: a hill glittering beside the way, the tempters' tune, a touchable glint. The child may touch it; Christian only looks and says "I will not go". Seen from further on: By-ends's party at its edge, then gone. [Hawthorne's parallel road; doubling]
- Lot's wife: a pale standing figure the child takes for a person, then for a stone. One touch shows the writing, "Remember Lot's wife", seen up close like the stone over the gate. [keepsakes]
- The River of the Water of Life and the meadow of lilies end this level in the book: a true rest, the first green since Level 3. [Journey's pacing: rest before the low point]

**13. By-path Meadow, Doubting Castle, Giant Despair**
- **The stile is the book's great choice**, and unlike the game's other choices it is Christian who gets it wrong. The soft path runs **beside** the hard one, in sight, for a long while; then night, rain, and the way back under water. [Hawthorne; Bunyan]
- **The Giant as shadow and tread**: found asleep at dawn by a shape that blocks the sky; in the dungeon he is a shadow across the grating and a voice. He has fits in sunshine, which is Bunyan's own softener. [shadow theatre; opera's Apollyon; the 2019 film as warning]
- Leave out the Giant's counsel that they end themselves and the beatings; keep "from Wednesday morning till Saturday night" as light crossing the wall four times. Owner's ruling.
- **The key called Promise** is the perfect touch act: at midnight the tray glints; the child touches Christian's chest ("I have a key in my bosom"); three doors, the last lock "went damnable hard": the child holds. [opera; Bunyan]
- They put up a warning pillar at the stile for those who come after. A quiet last act. [Sky's gifts left for others]
- Music: the helpers' phrase when the key turns; no hymn.

**14. The Delectable Mountains**
- Take Vaughan Williams's whole scene: **evening, three or four shepherds, a bird singing**, stillness. Friedrich for the picture. A level with almost no speech. [Shepherds of the Delectable Mountains]
- **The perspective glass**: Bunyan's own interaction. The shepherds hand them a glass to see the Celestial Gate from the hill called Clear; "their hands did shake" and they see only "something like the gate". The child drags a round lens over the far haze, and the image trembles and will not hold still. The destination is seen and not seen. [Bunyan; The Gardens Between's "look, don't act"; Journey's far mountain]
- The shepherd boy's song "He that is down need fear no fall" (Bunyan's words, Part Two), in a child's voice. [opera] (a departure: from Part Two)
- A panorama moment: the camera pans the whole range slowly, as the 1850 show unrolled. [Saco]

**15. Ignorance, the Flatterer, Atheist**
- The thinnest level in every adaptation; most cut it. Give it one strong picture: **the Flatterer's net**. A man "black of flesh, but covered with a very light robe" in the book; in the game a figure all in white, pleasant, with the tempters' tune, who leads them by a way that turns so slowly that they face away from the City before they notice (the far light slides from ahead to behind). Then the net falls as a soft dark web; a Shining One cuts it. [doubling; Hawthorne's smooth guide]
- **Atheist walks the other way**, back toward the start, laughing: the only person in the game going left on purpose. The glimpse through the glass in Level 14 is the answer ("Did we not see, from the Delectable Mountains, the gate of the city?"). [Bunyan; planting and pay-off]
- Ignorance comes in by "a little crooked lane" and lags behind: keep him a small figure always a little way back, to pay off in Level 17. [McLoughlin's exact-landing rule: not everyone who arrives enters]

**16. The Enchanted Ground**
- **The level that tries to stop the game.** The air is drowsy: colour drains to warm haze, the music slows and drops in pitch, his walk slows however the child holds, the word strip's text softens. Hopeful: "let us lie down here and take one nap". [Bunyan; GRIS's use of colour; Journey's snow]
- The cure is in the book: "let us fall into good discourse". Each time Hopeful speaks a line of his own story the picture clears a little. The child's act is to **touch Hopeful to keep him talking** when he flags; a no-fail rhythm of touch and clearing. [Florence's conversation mechanic]
- Arbours beside the road, soft and inviting, the tempters' tune at lullaby speed. [Hawthorne]
- Short. It should end by simply walking out into Beulah's light.

**17. Beulah, the River, the Celestial City**
- **Beulah**: Turner; birdsong; the panorama's most admired scene; the pilgrims "fell sick" with longing. A walk with no words. [Saco: Cropsey's Land of Beulah]
- **The River has no bridge and its depth changes with faith**: "deeper or shallower, as you believe in the King of the place". Christian sinks; Hopeful holds his chin up: "Be of good cheer, my brother: I feel the bottom, and it is good". Reuse Level 2's wading and the hand act: this time **the child's touch is Hopeful's hand under him**, the mirror of Help's hand. The water lowers as the far light grows. [Bunyan; planting from Level 2; opera]
- End it as the opera does: **trumpets and voices from the far side**, heard before seen; "all the bells in the city rang". [opera]
- The burden fell at the Cross; here "they had left their mortal garments behind them in the river". He climbs the hill "with ease".
- **The roll is handed in** at the gate: the tray's roll, carried since Level 5, touched one last time. [McLoughlin's exact landing; keepsakes]
- Amazing Grace was whole at the Cross; here the owner's rule says not to overplay it. Use the framing tune instead, or let the child's whole collection of notes ring as bells. [opera's "York" frame]
- Ignorance is ferried over by Vain-hope and turned away: Bunyan's hard last paragraph. Most children's versions drop it. Owner's ruling.
- Last picture: the dreamer wakes. Then the panorama ribbon of the whole road. [1912 film; opera; Saco]

## 9. Engagement without challenge: techniques from gentle games

Sources for the design claims: Journey's stated aim to make players feel "small, lonely and awed" along a planned emotional arc, with "no combat, no fail states, and no puzzles to gatekeep the story" ([GDC talk listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1017700/Designing), [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/pixel-pilgrim-exploring-journey), [The Ringer](https://www.theringer.com/2022/3/11/22970500/journey-jenova-chen-thatgamecompany-video-games-art)); Old Man's Journey as "a game for the reading nook", about 90 minutes, with a painted memory at the end of each stretch ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-broken-rules-i-old-man-s-journey-i-), [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/old_mans_journey/b/ios/archive/2017/05/31/a-poignant-adventure-through-memory)); A Short Hike, "simplicity is key; less can be more" ([GDC narrative review](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2024/GNR/Papers/Copas+GNR+-+A+Short+Hike.docx.pdf), [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/a-short-hike-best-game/)); Florence ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/moments-from-florence)). The rest is from my knowledge of the games ◇.

1. **A far goal always in view** (Journey's mountain). The game has the far light. Rule to add: when the light is hidden (Sinai, the Valley, the dungeon, the Flatterer's turn) that *is* the event; its return is the reward.
2. **A planned emotional curve with one low point** (Journey: rise, fall into the dark and snow, death, flight). Across 17 levels: joy at 5, lows at 9 and 13, the last low in the River. Never two dark levels without a bright one between: 8 and 9 are adjacent, so 7 and 10 must be warm.
3. **Colour comes back as the story heals** (GRIS). Already done at the Cross. Extend: each helper met leaves one colour in the palette that stays.
4. **The action is the meaning** (Florence: puzzle pieces get fewer as a talk gets easier). Each touch act should feel like its meaning: the hand, the knock, the key that turns hard, the glass that shakes, the shield held up.
5. **One new verb per level, used once or twice** (Monument Valley, Florence). The game does this; keep a list so no verb is repeated without a reason (the hand act returns in the River on purpose).
6. **Music that follows the player** (Journey's score bends to what you do; Sky). Footsteps already sing notes. Let pace matter: holding steadily keeps the tune whole; stopping lets it hang.
7. **A companion changes everything** (Journey, Sky, Spiritfarer). Faithful and Hopeful should be felt in play: two sets of footsteps, a second voice in the tune, and silence where Faithful's note was after Level 11.
8. **Loss without failure** (Spiritfarer's goodbyes; Journey's scarf torn in the snow). The roll lost in Level 6, Faithful in 11, light in 9. Something is taken and returned; nothing is failed.
9. **A place where control is gently reduced** (Journey's wind, the Slough's sinking). Already used; use it again sparingly: the Enchanted Ground's slowing, the River's depth.
10. **Staging by silhouette, depth and sound; the threat rarely shown** (Inside, Limbo). Apollyon, the lions, Giant Despair, the Valley: foreground shapes pass between us and him, the camera does the telling.
11. **Camera as storyteller** (Inside; Kentucky Route Zero's theatrical wide shots). The game's pull-backs are its best moments. One per level, at the picture the level was built for.
12. **Tactile small toys in the world** (Alba's photographs, Old Man's Journey's hills, Monument Valley's cranks). The game's birds, lizards, lamps and hidden notes. A rule of thumb: three in each level, one glint each the first time.
13. **Things you carry and can look at closely** (Unpacking, Behind the Frame). The tray: book, roll, key, coat, the glass, the certificate. Each new keepsake should tell its story when opened, and the Bible's ribbon could move to a new passage per level.
14. **A journal that fills by itself** (Alba's wildlife book, A Short Hike). The road-map inking in; no numbers, no percent.
15. **Short, whole sessions** (Florence's chapters, A Short Hike). Five to seven minutes a level, an end card that names what happened in the book's words. Good for a bedtime rhythm: one level a night.
16. **Wordless stretches** (Journey, GRIS, Old Man's Journey). After a talk-heavy level, a level with almost none: 14 and the start of 17.
17. **Time as the thing you touch** (The Gardens Between). A few moments where the child does not move him but moves the light: the days in the dungeon, the dawn at the end of the Valley, the glass on the hill.
18. **Planting and pay-off across hours** (Kentucky Route Zero, Journey's murals). The steps in the Slough, the Mountains seen from the Palace roof, the gate seen through the glass, Help's hand and Hopeful's hand, Ignorance lagging behind.
19. **A reason to walk it again** (Journey's white robe; Lewis). After the Cross: earlier levels without the burden, in tune, with one new thing to find. No new challenge, a changed world.
20. **Gifts left for those who come after** (Sky's candles and messages; the pillar at the stile). At the end of each level the child could leave one light at the road's side; on a replay it is there. No network is needed, it is the child's own.
21. **Idle beauty** (Ori, GRIS, Alba: the world moves when you stop). The game's rule is that nothing moves by itself *in the story*; weather, birds, smoke and light can. Standing still should always be worth it.
22. **Reading-nook tone** (Old Man's Journey). Never hurry the child. Hints are quiet and late.
23. **Sound first, sight second** for anything frightening or holy (the opera's offstage Shining Ones, Journey's chimes). The ear prepares the eye, and a 7-year-old can stop it by looking up from the phone.
24. **A motif for each side** (film scoring; the 1978 doubling). A helpers' phrase and a tempters' phrase, and the hymn kept apart from both.
25. **An ending that is given, not won** (Journey's last walk into light). The last level asks almost nothing of the child's hands: hold to walk, one hand to take, one roll to give.

## 10. Cautions

- **Do not let it trudge.** The 2019 film's worst review is the risk for Levels 10, 12 and 15.
- **Do not draw the fiends.** Every version that did was marked 8+ or older; the players start at 7.
- **Do not turn the book's fights into mechanics.** Both existing games did, and neither made a mark.
- **Do not cut the faltering.** The opera removed it and was called static. The Slough, the lost roll, the stile and the River are where children will care.
- **Preachiness** is the commonest complaint about every faithful version. The game's rule that the storyteller only tells the story, and the picture shows the rest, is the defence.
