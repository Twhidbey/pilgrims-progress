# Awe (f): the critic. Cohesion and restraint

The sixth researcher of the awe brief (`vision/awe-brief.md`). I read the brief, the five reports (`6-awe-scripture.md`, `7-awe-cross-choir.md`, `8-awe-light.md`, `9-awe-prayer.md`, `10-awe-nature.md`), their thirteen sketches in `concept/awe/`, the rules in CLAUDE.md, `vision/README.md` and `vision/5-levels-6-onward.md`, and checked the claims that matter against `book/pilgrims-progress-part-one.txt`. Nothing in the game was changed and nothing was spent. No sketch was drawn: the table in section 2 does the chart's work.

**The short of it.** The five reports are good and mostly true to the book. Their fault is the one the owner's rules warn against: each of them, alone, is restrained; together they put four or five "moments of awe" on the same few places (the Cross, the Valley, the dungeon, the Delectable Mountains, the River), claim the three remembered phrases of Amazing Grace five times over, and invent two different picture languages for remembered things. Below, every collision is resolved with one choice, each level is held to one or two special moments, and the best ten ideas are ranked.

---

## 1. One thing every later chat should know first: the hymn is already whole three times

The brief (and CLAUDE.md's notes on build 376 and build 390) say Amazing Grace is whole "only at the Cross and the Celestial Gate". **That is no longer true of the game.** Build 484 put the whole tune back at Level 1's city gate on the owner's word ("I loved amazing grace with lights"): `G.finale` in `index.html` plays every note of `TUNE` with its `BASS` as the gate swings open, one light going on ahead with each note. So the real shape is:

- **Found** (Level 1's gate: single soft notes, the lights going on ahead),
- **Given** (Level 5's Cross: slower, with bass and chords, after the silence),
- **Crowned** (Level 16's Celestial Gate: on the King's trumpets, the child playing it).

I recommend **keeping it** (the owner chose it, and found / given / crowned is a better arc than two) and **writing the rule that way** so no later chat "fixes" Level 1. Owner's yes needed only to restate the rule; nothing to build.

## 2. Collisions, and one choice for each

### 2.1 The three remembered phrases of Amazing Grace (claimed five times)

| Claimed by | Where |
|---|---|
| README / `5-levels-6-onward.md` (the owner's ruling, "the hymn whole at the end") and prayer (H) | Level 6 the roll found; Level 13 the key; Level 16 "I feel the bottom, and it is good" |
| light (7) | Level 9 the daybreak |
| nature (12) | Level 12 lying down by the River of Life |
| scripture (11) | Level 15 Hopeful's vision |

**Choice: the plan's three, unchanged: the roll (L6, `{109}`), the key (L13, `{292}`), the bottom of the River (L16, `{392}`).** They are all the same kind of moment (something lost or hoped for is given back), which is what the hymn's words are about, so a child learns one meaning for the tune. The Valley keeps its own psalm tune (Crimond, already planned) and nothing else; the River of Life and Hopeful's story get none.

### 2.2 The Cross (Level 5, built): kneel, choir, rays, a lark, a longer silence

Level 5 already holds the memories on the climb, the burden's fall, the whole hymn at sunrise, the close-up tears, the three Shining Ones, the sealed roll, the leaps and the song. It is the fullest level in the game. Proposed on top: the kneel and a longer silence (choir report 1, 3), the choir (choir 2), rays as the sun clears the beam (light 3), a lark after the leaps (nature 5).

**Choice: the kneel and the choir, nothing else.** Both are what the owner asked for in his own words ("a choir ... at the cross maybe him at his knees"). Rays and the lark are each lovely and each one thing too many: the dawn already rises exactly behind the crossing, and the first birdsong is better saved for after the Cross (the road in Level 6). Two cautions on the choir, which the choir report half-sees:

- **Music is punctuation.** As proposed, the hymn (about 27 s) runs straight into the choir (about 45 s): over a minute of unbroken music under eight spoken lines. Leave a breath of world-sound after the hymn's last chord; let the treble come as the first Shining One appears; let the choir **fall nearly silent under the talk** (not merely duck to .42) and swell once, as they go. Heard choir: under 20 s in all.
- **The kneel goes in the book's silence, not over a line.** The book: "I saw it no more. Then was Christian glad and lightsome, and said ..." (`{93}`, `{94}`), then "he stood still awhile", "as he stood looking and weeping". The choir report places it correctly (down in the silence, up before he speaks, standing for the tears). Keep exactly that; the tears close-up (build 449) stays standing.

### 2.3 The Valley of the Shadow (Level 9): four reports on one sunrise

Light (7, the great daybreak and the look back), prayer (C, All-prayer, the pool that does not grow), nature (9, the seven stars and Orion), scripture (6v, Psalm 23 heard, never seen).

**Choice: ONE sequence, in the book's order, built from all four:** All-prayer with the steady pool (prayer C, which rightly overturns the old plan's "the pool widens while you hold": a charging bar) → the voice ahead, Psalm 23, heard only (scripture) → the walls part on a narrow gap of sky with a few stars (nature) → the gap greys, then gold (light) → "He hath turned the shadow of death into the morning" → the look back (`{165}`). Stars and daybreak are one event in one gap, not two. **No remembered hymn phrase and no rays fan** (light's "strongest rays of the middle game": keep it to a slow soft lifting; the sketch's glare is too loud). One check on the book: Bunyan prints only the second half of Amos 5:8 at `{164}`; "the seven stars and Orion" is the verse's first half. Fair to picture, but it is our reading of his margin, not his words: list it.

### 2.4 Doubting Castle (Level 13): dawn in the slit, claimed by light, prayer and nature

Light (9: "one thin warm shaft through the slit ... and only then does Christian remember the key"; the child "holds ... and the slit slowly greys while they pray"), prayer (D: stars cross the slit, it pales, the wait does not depend on the finger), nature (13: a washed morning outside).

**Choice: prayer D.** Two corrections from the book: (1) the key comes **before** the day: "they began to pray, and continued in prayer till almost break of day. Now a little before it was day, good Christian ... brake out ... I have a Key in my bosom called Promise" (`{292}`). So the slit may only grey; the warm light belongs to stepping out of the last gate. Light's warm shaft before the key is an error. (2) Light's "holds and the slit greys while they pray" makes prayer a charge: the dark must pass by itself (prayer's rule). Nature's washed morning is then simply the light they walk out into: no extra beat, no rainbow.

### 2.5 The Delectable Mountains (Level 14): four claims

Nature (14, Palmer's ridges, the flock), scripture (10, the good Shepherd rising in the sky), light (10, the gate trembling in the glass), choir (4, a breath of the Cross's choir through the glass).

**Choice: the land is the level's picture; the glass is its one moment, with the faint choir inside it.** Cut the Shepherd snapshot: by scripture's own rule 3 ("where the book shows the thing itself, the place is the picture") the flock is real and in front of the child; a figure with a lamb over the real sheep is a greeting card. Let the touch on the flock (nature) carry "the sheep also are his" (`{296}`) when the Shepherd says it. The glass must always end in one clear second-long glimpse, whatever the child's hand does (light's own caution): the trembling is "the remembrance of that last thing ... made their hands shake" (`{305}`), not a test.

### 2.6 The River (Level 16): four claims, plus the Gate

Prayer (E: the cry, "Touch your friend", Hopeful's hand), light (14: the dark lifts on "Oh, I see him again!"), scripture (13: the Bible opening itself, Isaiah 43:2), nature (16: Beulah's dove before it), choir (5: the Gate).

**Choice: prayer E and light 14 are one moment** (the hand, then the light at the promise, `{392}`, `{393}`), with the third phrase on "I feel the bottom". Cut the Bible opening itself (one gesture too many at the end, as its author says). Beulah's dove stays (a different part of the level, quiet, the book's own `{382}`). The Gate is the ruled finale: trumpets (the hymn, played by the child), the bells, then "Holy, holy, holy" as they go in (choir 5). That is three musical things at once; the book does give all three (`{396}` to `{402}`), but they should come **one after another**, never stacked.

### 2.7 The Palace (Level 7): five ideas

Nature (7a the chamber Peace at sunrise, 7b the land seen far off from the roof), light (5, the same window), scripture (3 Daniel in the study, 4 Gideon's pitcher and the sling).

**Choice: the window dawn (quiet, the book's own `{131}`) and the roof view (the level's picture, `{137}`, Isa. 33:17, the promise Level 14 keeps).** At most ONE optional scripture touch in the armoury, Gideon's lamp in the pitcher (light hidden in clay, then shown, `{136}`): it speaks the game's own language of light. Daniel among lions in the sky, on the night he walked between the lions, doubles the fear the level has just spent; leave him in the records as words.

### 2.8 The lost roll (Level 6): prayer and light are the same idea

Prayer (B: he kneels, asks, the wait, goes back down, a line of light under the settle, thanks) and light (4: one low ray through the arbour's lattice onto the settle). **One idea; prayer's shape, light's single ray.** The spring at the hill's foot (nature 6, `{103}`, Isa. 49:10) is the book's ordinary act before the climb, not a second "moment": keep it plain, a drink and a ring on the water. Cut nature's first stars at the top (a second sky event in the same level). The ants (scripture 2, `{105}`) are a free easter egg; fine if missed.

### 2.9 After Apollyon (Level 8): three reports, one moment

Prayer (F: thanks first, then the hand with the leaves), light (6: the shadow slides off the sun, a trace of gold on the hand), nature (8: green flows back from where he sits). **All three together are one moment and agree.** Keep the order the book gives: thanks (`{153}`), then the hand (`{154}`). The green should be the River of Life's green (nature's own thread), and it is the only colour returning in the level.

### 2.10 Two picture languages for remembered things

Scripture proposes silver-gold snapshots out of the Bible ("silver = what the Book tells") beside Level 5's warm memories; light proposes Hopeful's vision as a column of the far light. **Choice: one language.** Remembered and told things use Level 5's `memPic()` look, and anything heavenly in them is drawn in the far light's own colours (white core, gold, silver reach). No second "silver memory" kind for a child to learn.

## 4. Tone for children 7 to 10

- **Grace as gift, not reward.** The prayer report gets this right and must become a rule: the wait never depends on the finger; nothing fills, grows or charges; the answer always comes from outside (a hand, a voice, a line of light). Two places in the other reports break it and are corrected above: light's dungeon ("holds ... and the slit greys while they pray") and the old Level 9 plan (the pool that widens while you hold). And the guardrail (prayer I): Help in the Slough and the burden at the Cross stay **unasked**. Never add a prayer beat before them.
- **Nothing preachy.** No new storyteller line explains prayer, light or the Bible. The TIP is "Stay with him", never "Pray". No sung words except "Holy, holy, holy" at the Gate (the book's own). Scripture is shown once (Hopeful) and otherwise heard in the book's own voice or left in the tray Bible to be found.
- **Nothing frightening made louder.** Cut: Lot's wife in the sky (it would also judge the child who chose "Stop and look back" at his own door in Level 1); Daniel's lions on the night of the lions; Elijah's chariot as the River's fear begins. The Valley's daybreak should come slowly (about 20 s from grey), not as a blaze of rays.
- **Never Christ's face or form.** Hopeful's "I saw the Lord Jesus Christ look down from heaven upon me" (`{350}`) is shown as light only. The good Shepherd figure is cut partly for this reason.
- **Sleepers who could read as bodies.** Two figures lying flat and dark in a meadow (the River of Life sketch) read at a glance as fallen men. In the game, draw them sitting against a tree or curled on their sides with the far light warm on them, and wake them by the hold.

## 8. Do not do

1. **No prayer meter.** Nothing grows, fills or brightens because the child holds longer (the old Level 9 pool; light's dungeon wording). The wait runs by itself.
2. **No prayer beat before unasked grace.** Help in the Slough and the burden at the Cross stay unasked.
3. **No more than three remembered phrases** after the Cross (roll, key, River bottom). None at the Valley, the River of Life or Hopeful's story. And do not "fix" Level 1's whole hymn at the gate: restate the rule as found / given / crowned.
4. **Nothing more at the Cross** than the kneel and the choir: no rays fan, no lark, no sung words, no Latin motets, no minute of unbroken music.
5. **No judgment pictures in the sky:** no Lot's wife (it judges Level 1's "Stop and look back"), no Daniel's lions on the night of the lions, no Elijah's chariot at the River, no furnace beside Faithful.
6. **Never Christ's face or form**, and no good Shepherd figure over real sheep.
7. **No second picture language** for remembered things (no "silver snapshots"); one memory look, lit by the far light when heavenly.
8. **No rainbow.**
9. **No warm light before the key** in the dungeon (the book: "a little before it was day").
10. **No Bible opening itself unasked** at the River; the tray Bible only opens when the child opens it.
11. **No wholesale re-lighting of Levels 1 to 5** for the shared far light without before-and-after stills, one level at a time.
12. **No museum of touches** in the Palace: one optional object at most.
13. **No sleepers who read as bodies**, and no Flatterer drawn to frighten.
