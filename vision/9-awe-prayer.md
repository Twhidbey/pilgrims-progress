## 1. What the book actually does with prayer

Read straight through, Part One has about a dozen moments where a pilgrim cries out, prays or gives thanks and help comes. Four things about them matter for the game more than any single scene:

1. **Help often comes before anyone asks.** In the Slough the book gives Christian no prayer at all: "a man came to him, whose name was Help" (`{31}`). At the Cross the burden falls "of itself" (`{68}`, `{94}`). Bunyan does not make prayer a deal where you ask and then get. Grace comes first, prayer follows, and the game should show both.
2. **Many answers come slowly.** In Doubting Castle they "began to pray, and continued in prayer till almost break of day" (`{292}`). Hopeful tells how he prayed "over, and over, and over" and was answered "not at the first, nor second, nor third ... no, nor at the sixth time neither", and kept on because "though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come" (`{349}`). Waiting is part of the story. A prayer that works the instant you press it would be untrue to the book.
3. **The answer comes from outside him and is something he receives:** a hand (Evangelist `{52}`, Help `{32}`, the hand with the leaves `{154}`, Hopeful holding his head up `{392}`), a voice (`{163}`, `{282}`, `{393}`), an opened door (`{60}`, `{292}`), or his eye led to a lost thing (`{109}`: "gave thanks to God for directing his eye to the place where it lay").
4. **Thanks follows.** "I will here give thanks to him that delivered me" (`{153}`). "Gave thanks to God" (`{109}`). Three leaps and a song (`{94}`). The book's prayers have two ends: the cry and the thanks.

The game's own grammar for grace already fits this: **hands**. Help's hand can be taken only after "Give me your hand" has been said and is fully out; Christian reaches only once the child touches it (build 453). Evangelist's hand follows "Woe is me" (build 366). Good-will's pull follows the knocking (build 404). The burden at the Cross falls without the child (build 390). I am proposing one more piece of that grammar, not a new system.

## 3. One gesture across the game: ask, wait, receive, give thanks

**The rule.** Prayer is never a button and never a meter. It is a short pause in which the child keeps Christian company, and the answer is always a **gift that comes from outside him, in its own time**, which the child then **receives** with the same touch the game already uses for Help's hand.

**The four beats (all from the book's own shape, `{108}`-`{109}`, `{153}`-`{154}`, `{292}`-`{293}`):**

1. **Ask.** The book's cry is said, in his own voice (or the storyteller's, as the book has it). A quiet TIP follows, the same words every time: **"Stay with him."** (not "Pray", which would tell a child what to believe; not "Hold to pray", which turns it into an action). The child rests a finger on him (a hold, as walking already is). He kneels (`christian-kneel.pack`, `KNEEL`, played forward to its breathing frames 50 to 95; played backward later, it is his rising), or, where he must stay on his feet (the Valley), he stops and bows his head (`P.lookA` tilted down instead of up, the trick Level 1 already uses).
2. **Wait.** The world grows quiet: the music, the wind and the rain sink to a whisper (as the pause menu's `KS.paused` whisper does), and nothing in the picture moves but him breathing. **The wait does not depend on the finger.** It has a fixed length set by the story (about 3 to 6 seconds; longer and shown as passing time in the dungeon), and it runs whether the child holds or lets go. Holding only keeps the world hushed and him kneeling (if the child lets go he stays kneeling a little while anyway, as the book says "continued in prayer"). Nothing fills up, nothing glows brighter the longer you hold, and there is no "charge". This is what keeps it from becoming a mechanic that wins.
3. **Receive.** The answer comes **from the side of the far light** (the light he has followed since Level 1, which the README calls the game's best idea), every time: a hand reaches in from that side, a voice comes from ahead, or a thin line of that light finds something (the roll under the settle, the key at his breast, the bottom of the river). Only once the gift is fully there (Help's rule, build 453: the hand out AND the words finished), the gold corner prompt shows ("take his hand ✦", "touch the key ✦"), and one touch receives it.
4. **Thanks.** A short beat after: he rises (the kneel backward), and where the book gives thanks words (`{109}`, `{153}`) he says them, short. Where it gives none, the thanks is shown: he lifts his head to the light, as at the Cross (`P.lookUp`).

**One sound for it, used nowhere else.** As the wait begins, a single very soft low sustained tone (sine, a breath of a fifth above, the old `padG` kind) that simply stops when the answer comes, so the answer arrives into silence. When an answer is one of the three "remembered phrases" of Amazing Grace allowed after the Cross (the brief's rule), it plays then; I suggest giving all three to answered prayers (see idea H).

**Where to use it: only four times** (the lost roll, the Valley, the dungeon, the River). Not at every moment of help. Help in the Slough and the burden at the Cross stay unasked, so the child learns that grace is not only given in reply.

**What prayer looks like to a 7 to 10 year old**, and how the gesture meets it: kneeling or bowing, hands together, being quiet, sometimes waiting and not getting an answer straight away, and saying thank you afterwards. Children do this at bedtime and in school assemblies. They also know prayer from people being sad and asking for help, which is exactly when the book has Christian pray. What would put them off: being told what to feel, a narrator explaining what prayer is, or prayer as a power-up. So: no new storyteller line explains anything, the TIP never says "pray", and the gift is never a score.

## 4. The ideas

### A. The Quiet (the gesture above), as the rule for Levels 6 to 16
- **Where:** a rule in CLAUDE.md's "Rules for play" once agreed; first used in Level 6.
- **Sees / hears / does:** as section 3.
- **Why true:** the book's prayers have this shape: cry, waiting, a gift from outside, thanks (`{108}`-`{109}`, `{153}`-`{154}`, `{292}`-`{293}`, `{391}`-`{393}`).
- **Cost:** free (the kneel pack exists, but with the burden: see "Figures" below).
- **Risk:** **overuse** (keep it to four moments); **mechanic creep** (a future chat makes the light grow with hold time: the rule must say no); the wait is dead air if too long (cap it at 6 s outside the dungeon).
- **Book or departure:** the cries, waits, answers and thanks are the book's; the child's resting finger and the TIP are ours (D, as every child's act is).

### B. Level 6: the lost roll found (`{107}` to `{109}`): the first use, and the most childlike
- **Sees:** at the top of Hill Difficulty, after Timorous and Mistrust run back, he "felt in his bosom for his roll ... and found it not" (`{107}`). The tray's roll fades out (the tray the child has been touching since Level 1). The storyteller: "He knew not what to do." He kneels. **Ask:** "Then he fell down upon his knees, and asked God's forgiveness for his foolish act." (`{108}`, made plain). TIP "Stay with him." Wait, about four seconds of hush. Then he rises by himself and goes **back down** to the arbour (hold to walk, walking the wrong way and slower, as the book has it: "the sorrow of Christian's heart ... Sometimes he sighed, sometimes he wept"). **Receive:** at the arbour he sits and weeps, then a thin line of the far light falls under the settle and the roll glints there (it does not glow by itself: his eye is "directed"). Touch it: it rises into the tray. **Thanks:** "He gave thanks to God for directing his eye to the place where it lay." He goes back up "nimbly", and "the sun went down upon Christian" (`{109}`).
- **Why it is the best first use:** every child has lost something precious and looked for it, and has known the feeling of finding it. No monster is needed and no fear. And the book itself makes the finding God's answer and the thanks his.
- **Cost:** free (the kneel pack; the roll and the tray exist; the arbour and the settle drawn in code). Voices: about 4 lines, about 4 cents, only on a yes.
- **Risk:** the long walk back down could drag: keep it short and add the light falling under the settle as the moment the child waits for.
- **B / D:** all book except the shaft of light under the settle (D: the book says only that his eye was directed) and the roll fading from the tray (D, a game device).

### C. Level 9: All-prayer in the Valley (`{161}` to `{166}`)
- **Sees:** the darkest screen of the game (section 4 of `5-levels-6-onward.md`), one small pool of light round him. At the burning pit: "Now, thought Christian, what shall I do?" (`{161}`, the same words as Level 1's first cry: let the child hear the echo). The storyteller: "He put up his sword, and took another weapon, called All-prayer." The sword in the tray dims. He stops, bows his head, his own voice: "O Lord, I beseech thee, deliver my soul!" TIP "Stay with him."
- **Change from the earlier plan:** `5-levels-6-onward.md` proposed "the pool of light round him widens while the child holds: holding is praying". I advise against the widening: it is a charging bar, and the book says the flames went on "reaching towards him" for "a great while" after he prayed. Instead the pool **stays as it is** (prayer keeps him, it does not make the dark go away), and he can walk on while praying (in the book he "went on a great while"): holding now walks him slowly, head bowed. The answer comes in two parts, each from outside: (1) as the dark thickens ahead, "I will walk in the strength of the Lord God!" (the child's one touch on him, as in the earlier plan), "so they gave back"; (2) later, unasked, **the voice ahead**: "Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for thou art with me" (`{163}`), and a faint second light far ahead (Faithful, not named). Then "by and by the day broke" (`{164}`).
- **Thanks:** "He hath turned the shadow of death into the morning." The look back at sunrise (`{165}`) is the level's great picture.
- **Cost:** free in code; voices already counted in the Level 9 plan.
- **Risk:** fear. The gentle telling of the earlier report stands (sound and dark only; the whisperer cut, or told in one line for an anxious child: "he thought the wicked words were his own", which can comfort; owner's ruling).
- **B / D:** all book except his walking with head bowed (D, staging) and the second light ahead (D: the book has only a voice).
- **Sketch:** `concept/awe/prayer-valley-held-light.jpg` (below).

### D. Level 13: prayer till almost break of day (`{292}`, `{293}`): the level's great moment
- **Sees:** the dungeon is all black but ONE slit of window high up (the earlier plan has its bar of light crossing the floor once a day, Wednesday to Saturday). Saturday night: the slit is black. The storyteller: "On Saturday, about midnight, they began to pray, and continued in prayer till almost break of day." TIP "Stay with them." The two kneel facing each other (Christian and Hopeful), a breath of hush, and then **the night passes in the slit itself:** two or three stars drift slowly across it (about 12 s, the only long wait in the game), the slit pales from black to grey to the first rose, and a faint flat pool of that grey lies on the floor between them. Nothing the child does makes it faster: the night is shown honestly as long.
- **Receive:** just before day, the faintest glint at Christian's breast. The book's turn is the most childlike line in Part One: "What a fool am I, to lie in a dungeon, when I may as well walk at liberty! I have a key in my bosom, called Promise" (`{292}`): the answer was with him all along. Hopeful: "That's good news, good brother. Pluck it out of thy bosom, and try." The key comes into the tray (if it has been lying there since the Cross, as the earlier arc proposes, the child has seen it every day and not known: the child feels the same foolish gladness he does). Touch it: three locks (the third must be held: "went damnable hard", `{293}`), the gate opens on Sunday's sun.
- **Why:** Bunyan sets the escape at daybreak on a Sunday (Resurrection morning); the long prayer and the slow light are his. It is also the clearest place to show a child that an answer can come slowly, without a word of explanation.
- **Cost:** free in code; Hopeful kneeling needs a figure (see Figures).
- **Risk:** the Giant's cruelty (the owner's ruling, `5-levels-6-onward.md`); the twelve-second night could feel like waiting for nothing (so the stars must visibly move and the slit visibly pale: the waiting must look beautiful).
- **B / D:** all book; the stars in the slit, the glint and the night shown passing are ours (D, staging).
- **Sketch:** `concept/awe/prayer-dungeon-break-of-day.jpg` (below).

### E. Level 16: the River, the cry and the hand (`{391}` to `{393}`)
- **Sees:** Level 2's wading again (same act, same clip, deliberately). In the middle he sinks: "I sink in deep waters; the billows go over my head" (`{391}`), the dark comes (Level 2's `darkMist`), and the hold moves him less and less, as in the Slough. **No "Stay with him" here:** this is the one cry where the child's part is the friend's: TIP "Touch your friend." Hopeful's hand (Help's rule: out, words said, then touch). "Be of good cheer, my brother: I feel the bottom, and it is good." Then, after a pause, "Oh, I see him again!" and the promise ("When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee", `{393}`) is said, the dark lifts from the far shore toward him, and "he found ground to stand upon".
- **Why:** the River rhymes with the Slough in the book (the README's point 6); in the Slough Help came unasked, in the River the friend answers his cry. The child has learned the gesture by now and here gives it.
- **Cost / risk:** in the Level 16 plan. Risk: the River is death; the gentle telling stands.
- **B / D:** all book except the child's touch (D, as Help's).

### F. Level 8: thanks first, then the hand with the leaves (`{153}`, `{154}`)
- **Sees:** after Apollyon has gone, he kneels (the kneel pack) and says, short: "I will here give thanks to him that delivered me." (`{153}`; his verse with "blessed Michael" left out, as the earlier report advised.) Only after the thanks, a hand comes in from the far light's side holding leaves. Touch it: the red goes out of the picture and his wounds (if shown at all) are healed.
- **Why:** the order matters: here thanks comes first and the gift after it. It keeps the gesture from being only "ask and get".
- **Cost:** free in code. **Risk:** small. **B / D:** book; the hand coming from the far light's side is ours.

### G. The family he prayed for (`{11}`, then Part Two): the quietest answer, at the very end
- **Sees:** nothing in Levels 1 to 15. On the book's last contents page after "So I awoke" (the earlier report's idea), one line: "Now it came to pass that his wife, and their four boys, went after him. But that is another dream."
- **Why it belongs to this theme:** the book's first prayer is his prayer for his family ("to pray for and pity them", `{11}`), and the game opens on his wife in the lit door. Part Two answers it. A child who has worried about that door since the first screen gets the answer last, without a sermon: an answer that came after a very long time, after he had gone.
- **Optional, free:** in Level 1, at the door, if the child chooses "Stop and look back", he could bow his head a moment before he runs on (the prayer of `{11}`, shown, not said). D.
- **Cost:** free (one quiet line, about a cent if spoken; better unspoken, in the title page's italic).
- **Risk:** it is outside Part One: owner's choice (also raised in `5-levels-6-onward.md`).

### H. The three remembered phrases of Amazing Grace, each on an answered prayer
- **Where:** the brief allows three single remembered phrases after the Cross. Give them to: (1) the roll found under the settle (`{109}`, Level 6); (2) the key at break of day (`{292}`, Level 13); (3) "I feel the bottom, and it is good" (`{392}`, Level 16). Then it is whole only at the Celestial Gate.
- **Why:** each is the moment something lost or hoped for is given back; the hymn is about exactly that ("I once was lost, but now am found"). The child will link the tune with being answered.
- **Cost:** free (Level 1's `TUNE` notes, one phrase each). **Risk:** clashes with the Valley's Crimond (the earlier plan); keep Crimond for the Valley's voice only, as its own tune.

### I. Leave the unasked graces alone (a guardrail, not a feature)
- Help in the Slough (`{31}`) and the burden at the Cross (`{94}`) must not be given a prayer beat later by a well-meaning change. If every rescue followed a prayer, the game would teach "pray to unlock help", which the book does not. Write this into the rule.

## 5. My top three

1. **A. The Quiet: one gentle gesture (ask, wait, receive, give thanks), used only four times, with a wait that does not depend on the finger.** It makes prayer part of the story's grammar of hands and gifts without turning it into a button, and it fixes the one risk in the existing Level 9 plan (a pool of light that grows while you hold).
2. **D. Doubting Castle's prayer till almost break of day**, the night passing in one slit of window, the key glinting at his breast: the clearest picture of a slow answer in the game, and Bunyan's Sunday daybreak.
3. **B. The lost roll found (Level 6)**: the first and smallest use, the most childlike (losing something precious, kneeling, looking, finding, saying thank you), free with the existing kneel pack, and it comes first so the child learns the gesture where nothing is frightening.
