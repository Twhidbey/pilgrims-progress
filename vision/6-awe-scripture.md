# Awe (a): Scripture snapshots

Research for the awe-and-reverence brief (`vision/awe-brief.md`). **Theme:** short Bible scenes shown as "thought pictures", using Level 5's memories in the sky (`cross.js`: `MEMS`, `memPic()`, `memories()`: dark warm figures in a soft oval of light that melts into the sky, then fades). Nothing in the game was changed and nothing was spent. Sketches are in `concept/awe/scripture-*.jpg`.

**How this was done.** Every margin reference Bunyan prints in Part One (223 of them, the `[..]` marks in `book/pilgrims-progress-part-one.txt`) was listed with its section number, then read in its place. An idea counts as "Bunyan's own" only when his margin (or his text) names that Bible story at that moment. Old-master references marked ◇ are from memory and could not be opened from here: check them on Wikimedia Commons before quoting.

---

## 1. The rules I would set for snapshots (restraint first)

1. **Few.** At most one in a level, and none in most levels. Over Levels 6 to 16 I recommend **four**, five at most. The Cross already has his six memories; a second kind of picture in the sky must stay rare or it becomes wallpaper.
2. **Only where Bunyan points.** Each one stands on a margin reference (or a Bible name in his own text) at that very moment. No snapshot is ours alone.
3. **Where the book shows the thing itself, the place is the picture.** Sinai is Level 3's mountain; the pillar of salt stands in Level 12's scene; the Shepherds' sheep are real sheep; the River is a real river. No snapshot is laid over these.
4. **In the dark, scripture is heard, never seen.** In the Valley of the Shadow, Doubting Castle, the storm at By-path and the River, the Bible comes as a voice (as Bunyan has it: "he thought he heard the voice of a man ... saying, Though I walk ..."). Pictures belong to the lit places: the Palace, the Mountains, the Enchanted Ground's long talk.
5. **Never Christ's face or form.** The game's cross is empty (the owner's choice, build 411). Where Bunyan has a vision of Christ (Hopeful, `{350}`), show only light from above on the one who prays.
6. **Mercy pictures, not judgment pictures.** No Lot's wife turning to salt, no fiery furnace beside Faithful's death, no flood, no Pharaoh. Show the hand that leads out, the lamp, the shepherd, the light.
7. **Unspoken.** A snapshot adds no line. It comes on a line already in the script (Bunyan's own), or the child calls it up. Never over another voice (Level 5's rule: `busy()`, a gap of quiet before and after).
8. **One simple tableau, two or three figures, held about 3 to 5 seconds.** Silhouettes from an old composition, as Level 5's memories are; no faces.
9. **Told apart from his memories.** His memories (Level 5) are his own life, warm. Scripture snapshots rise **out of the Bible in the tray**: the Bible glints first, and the oval is a touch paler, a silver-gold, as if lit by the page. So a child learns: warm = what happened to him; silver = what the Book tells.

### The way they come: the Bible opens itself (free, and the child chooses)

His Bible already lives in the tray (`keepsakes.js`: Romans 3 and 4, Hebrews 9 and 10, Revelation 21, his pen notes, the ribbon). Proposal: at the four snapshot places the Bible's corner glints once (`glint()` exists in `hill.js`). **If the child touches it**, the book opens at Bunyan's margin verse for that moment (a new spread, his ribbon there, a pen note in his hand), and as it opens the snapshot rises off the page into the sky, holds, and melts; the book closes by itself. If the child does not touch it, nothing is lost and the story goes on. This keeps the snapshot a discovery (like the hymn's lights), never a lesson, and makes the tray Bible matter across the whole game. Every new verse checked word for word against `es-kjv` and `bible-kjv` (the notes' rule).

## 2. The idea list, level by level

Levels 1 to 5 are built and full; I recommend **no snapshots there** (the Cross's memories are the high point and should stay alone). Two free touches only, both in the tray Bible.

### Levels 1 to 5 (tray only)

**1a. Level 2, after Help's lift: Psalm 40 in his Bible.** WHERE `{32}`: "he drew him out, and set him upon sound ground, and bid him go on his way. [Ps. 40:2]". WHAT a new spread, Psalm 40:1-3 ("He brought me up also out of an horrible pit, out of the miry clay, and set my feet upon a rock"), "miry clay" underlined, beside it in his hand "the Slough!". No picture, no voice; found only by a child who opens the book in Level 3 on. WHY Bunyan's own margin. COST free. RISK none; it is quiet.

**1b. Level 1's margin at the run, kept for Level 12.** At `{18}`, "So he looked not behind him, but fled towards the middle of the plain", Bunyan's margin is **Gen. 19:17** (the angel to Lot: "look not behind thee"). Do NOT add a picture to Level 1 (the run is already full of music and family, and the child may choose "Stop and look back" without blame). Instead Level 12 answers it (idea 6).

### Level 6, the Hill Difficulty (`{95}` to `{109}`)

**2. The ant at the arbour (nature, not a snapshot).** WHERE `{105}`, the arbour, where he sleeps and loses the roll; the one who wakes him says "Go to the ant, thou sluggard; consider her ways, and be wise" [Prov. 6:6]. WHAT a line of real ants carrying seed across the bench by his hand as he wakes, two seconds, an easter egg; the line is Bunyan's. COST free (code). RISK too small to see on a phone; it is fine if missed.
No snapshot in Level 6 (John 10:1 at Formalist and Hypocrisy, `{98}`, is a lesson, not a picture).

### Level 7, the lions and the Palace Beautiful (`{110}` to `{140}`): the richest place

The Palace's own story is a house of records and an armoury of Bible things. Bunyan lists them himself, so here a snapshot is not a departure in kind, only in form.

**3. Daniel among the lions, in the study (recommended).** WHERE `{133}`: "they read to him some of the worthy acts ... how they had ... stopped the mouths of lions, quenched the violence of fire ... [Heb. 11:33,34]". He has walked between the chained lions that very evening (`{110}`, `{111}`). WHAT in the candlelit study a damsel opens the records; the child touches the open book; a snapshot rises in the window's dark: one man kneeling, hands together, a high window's shaft of light on him, two great lions lying down either side of him, quiet (sketch, top left of the sheet). Silent; the storyteller's line about the records is enough. WHY Bunyan's margin, and it rhymes with what the child has just lived: the lions he passed were chained; Daniel's were shut. COST free (the kneel pack and code lions; a painted plate optional). RISK lions frighten little children: show them lying down, heads on paws, the man calm. Reference: Doré, *Daniel in the Den of Lions* (1866) ◇; Rembrandt's drawing of Daniel in the lions' den ◇; Schnorr von Carolsfeld, *Die Bibel in Bildern* (1860) ◇.

**4. The armoury: touch Gideon's pitcher (recommended), the sling (optional).** WHERE `{136}`: "They shewed him Moses' rod; the hammer and nail with which Jael slew Sisera; the pitchers, trumpets, and lamps too, with which Gideon put to flight the armies of Midian ... the jaw-bone ... the sling and stone with which David slew Goliath". WHAT a wall of the armoury by candle (sword, shield, All-prayer as he is given them, `{135}`); on a shelf three old things only: Moses' rod, a clay pitcher with a lamp glowing in it, a sling (sketch `scripture-armoury-gideon.jpg`). Touching the pitcher: the snapshot of Gideon's men on a dark hill, each holding a lamp high, a row of small lights in the night. Touching the sling: a small figure before a great dark one (sets up Apollyon next level). One touch each, then they rest. WHY Bunyan's own list. Leave out Jael's hammer and nail and the jaw-bone (violent). COST free. RISK becoming a museum of touches: allow two objects only, and never make them required. References: Doré, *Gideon* plates ◇ and *David and Goliath* ◇; Schnorr von Carolsfeld ◇.

**5. From the roof: Isaiah 33:17 (nature, heard).** WHERE `{137}`: they show him the Delectable Mountains far off [Isa. 33:16,17: "Thine eyes shall see the king in his beauty: they shall behold the land that is very far off"]. Belongs to the nature researcher; no snapshot. The verse could be the one his Bible opens to here if a tray page is wanted.

### Level 8, Apollyon (`{140}` to `{154}`)

**No snapshot.** Christian's own words carry it: "Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy; when I fall I shall arise" [Micah 7:8], `{151}`. A flash of David with his sling as he catches the sword was considered and **turned down**: it would clutter the game's most violent moment and explain what the child can feel. The sling seen the night before (idea 4) is the quiet setup.

### Level 9, the Valley of the Shadow (`{155}` to `{168}`): heard only

**6v. The voice ahead, Psalm 23:4.** `{163}`: "he thought he heard the voice of a man, as going before him, saying, Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for thou art with me." Already in the Level 9 plan. Keep it a voice in the dark, never a picture. Then at daybreak Christian's own two verses, `{164}` "He hath turned the shadow of death into the morning" [Amos 5:8] and `{166}` "His candle shineth upon my head, and by his light I walk through darkness" [Job 29:3]: spoken, as the first sunrise he walks into comes. King David in the quag (`{159}`, told by the storyteller) stays a sentence, not a picture. Tray: Psalm 23 can be the page his Bible holds from here on.

### Level 10 and 11, Faithful, Vanity Fair

**No snapshot.** Faithful's stories (Adam the First, Moses who "spareth none", `{178}`) are his own memories and frightening ones; if pictured at all they are Faithful's memories (warm, Blake's floating figures), not scripture. At the trial (`{238}`) the charge cites Daniel 3 and 6, the furnace and the lions' den: **do not picture them** beside Faithful's death; a furnace rescue there would promise a rescue that comes another way (the chariot).

### Level 12, By-ends, the silver mine, Lot's wife, the River of Life (`{245}` to `{276}`)

**7. Led out by the hand (recommended, the one warning snapshot, told as mercy).** WHERE `{272}`: the pillar with "Remember Lot's Wife", "for her looking back with a covetous heart, when she was going from Sodom for safety [Gen. 19:26]". The pillar is in the scene itself (rule 3). WHAT as Christian reads the words out, a snapshot rises above the pillar: two figures hurrying right toward a dawn light, an angel's hand leading them (Gen. 19:16, "the men laid hold upon his hand"); behind them a third, paler figure standing still and turned back toward a low red glow; nothing more (sketch, bottom middle of the sheet: there the angel is left out). Hopeful then says his own line, "I am not now as Lot's wife" (shortened from `{273}`). WHY it answers Level 1, where Bunyan's margin at the run is Gen. 19:17, and the game's "Stop and look back" choice. Hands are the game's language of grace. COST free. RISK (a) judgment: show only the standing still, never the change to salt; (b) a child who chose "Stop and look back" in Level 1 must not feel judged: Bunyan's point is the covetous heart, and Christian did not go back; the storyteller says nothing about Level 1. Reference: Doré, *The Flight of Lot* ◇; Dürer's *Lot and his Daughters fleeing Sodom* (c. 1498, a panel with Sodom burning behind) ◇.

**8. The River of Life (heard).** `{276}` [Ps. 23:2, Rev. 22, Ezek. 47]: the scene is the river; at most his Bible opens at Psalm 23 again, "He leadeth me beside the still waters", a remembered page from the Valley.

### Level 13, By-path Meadow and Doubting Castle: heard only

**9. The voice in the storm.** `{282}`: "they heard the voice of one saying, Set thine heart toward the highway, even the way which thou wentest; turn again [Jer. 31:21]". A voice in the rain, never a picture. In the dungeon nothing is shown; the key called Promise is the answer.

### Level 14, the Delectable Mountains (`{295}` to `{306}`)

**10. The good Shepherd (recommended).** WHERE `{296}`: "These mountains are Immanuel's Land ... and the sheep also are his, and he laid down his life for them [John 10:11]." WHAT the four Shepherds stand with their real flock; as the first says this line, a snapshot rises over the far ridge where the sun is: one figure with a lamb across his shoulders, sheep at his feet (sketch, bottom left of the sheet). It points back to the empty cross without showing it. Silent. WHY Bunyan's margin, and his own Shepherds speak it. COST free. RISK it can look like a greeting card; keep it small, high, brief, in the Mountains' fullest colour so it is the land's own light. Reference: the early Christian *Good Shepherd* (catacomb of Callixtus fresco, 3rd century, and the marble statue in the Vatican Museo Pio Cristiano) ◇: the oldest Christian picture of Christ is this one, with no face to speak of; and **Rembrandt's etching *The Angel Appearing to the Shepherds* (1634)**: shepherds and flock under a great shaft of light from heaven, the master for this level's light.

### Level 15, the Flatterer and the Enchanted Ground (`{307}` to `{381}`)

**11. Hopeful's answered prayer (recommended, my first choice).** WHERE `{348}` to `{351}`. To keep awake on the Enchanted Ground Christian asks Hopeful how he came to believe. Hopeful: Faithful told him "I must entreat upon my knees ... the Father to reveal him to me [Ps. 95:6, Dan. 6:10, Jer. 29:12,13]"; "So I continued praying until the Father showed me his Son"; "I did not see him with my bodily eyes, but with the eyes of my understanding [Eph. 1:18,19] ... One day I was very sad ... suddenly, as I thought, I saw the Lord Jesus Christ look down from heaven upon me, and saying, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved [Acts 16:30,31] ... Then the water stood in mine eyes". WHAT as Hopeful tells it, his memory rises in the hazy sky: a man kneeling alone, and one shaft of light coming straight down on him from above; no figure in the light (sketch `scripture-hopeful-prayer.jpg`). Held while he says one plain line (from `{350}`, shortened): "One day I was very sad. I prayed, and I saw him look down from heaven upon me." Then the snapshot melts, and the drowsy haze of the Enchanted Ground lifts a little. WHY it is the book's own vision and the owner's "an answered prayer, light from heaven" exactly; it is told, so it is truly a thought picture; and **Acts 16:30-31 is the very margin of Christian's first cry in Level 1** (`{10}`, "What shall I do to be saved?"): the game's first question gets its answer here, in a friend's story. COST free (the kneel pack as a stand-in until Hopeful's own kneel exists; his walk is in the Level 11 plan). Voice: the one line, about a cent. RISK (a) showing Christ: avoided, only light; (b) a sermon: one line, no more; (c) the long talk around it must be cut hard (the Level 15 plan already says so). This is also a natural place for the **second remembered phrase of Amazing Grace** (the vision plan allows three after the Cross): one phrase on the light, "I once was lost", sung by no one, as a single soft line of notes; owner's call.

### Level 16, Beulah, the River, the City (`{382}` to `{403}`)

**12. Enoch and Elijah (optional, I lean against).** `{390}`: "there hath not any, save two, to wit, Enoch and Elijah, been permitted to tread that path". A small chariot of fire rising (sketch, bottom right of the sheet). Turned down for now: it sits just as the River's fear begins and would answer it with a way out that is not given; and Faithful's chariot (Level 11) already owns that picture. Heard instead: the Shining Ones' line as Bunyan has it.

**13. In the River, heard.** `{393}`: Christian, sinking, "Oh, I see him again! and he tells me, When thou passest through the waters, I will be with thee [Isa. 43:2]". A voice, with Hopeful's hand (Help's hand returned). No picture: he sees, the child hears. The Bible in the tray could open itself here, the only time it does so unasked, at Isaiah 43:2, and then close for good as the roll is given in. Owner's call; it may be one gesture too many at the end.

## 3. My top three

1. **Hopeful's answered prayer, Level 15** (`{350}`): one kneeling man under one shaft of light, told in his own words. The owner's "light from heaven, an answered prayer" is Bunyan's own scene, and it answers the game's first cry (Acts 16:30-31 at `{10}` and at `{350}`).
2. **The Palace's records and armoury, Level 7** (`{133}`, `{136}`): Daniel resting among lions (the night he passed the lions himself), and Gideon's lamps in the night (light hidden in clay, then shown), called up by a touch. Bunyan lists the very things; it is the one place in the book that is a museum of Bible stories.
3. **The good Shepherd over the Delectable Mountains, Level 14** (`{296}`): the oldest Christian picture of Christ, faceless and gentle, rising where real shepherds say "he laid down his life for them": the Cross remembered without being shown again.

Close fourth: **Lot led out by the hand, Level 12** (`{272}`), told as mercy, answering Level 1.

Total proposed: four snapshots after the Cross, one each in Levels 7, 12, 14, 15 (Level 7 may have two, both optional touches); every one silent but Hopeful's, which has one line. Every dark place keeps its scripture as a voice.

## 5. Cost

All of it can be free: the snapshots are built like `memPic()` from existing packs and code shapes; the tray spreads are text. Paid only if the owner wants painted plates for them (below), and Hopeful's one line (about a cent). Real figures for Hopeful (walk, kneel) are already needed by Levels 11 to 16 and are counted there.

### An image prompt, written, not run (gemini-3-pro-image, 16:9, 2K, about 15 cents)

Only if the code silhouettes look too crude once built. One sheet; each tableau cut out later and toned with `memPic()`'s own steps.

> Six small separate scenes on one sheet of plain white paper, arranged in two rows of three with wide white space between them, each painted as a single soft monochrome watercolour silhouette in warm dark umber, like a shadow-puppet play: a completely FLAT SIDE VIEW, every figure seen from the side, both feet on one ground line, NO faces (no eyes, no mouth, the heads a plain soft wash), no text, no frames, no background beyond a short strip of ground under each. Style: the quiet silhouettes of the reference figure (same brush, same weight), with the composition of old Bible engravings by Gustave Doré and Julius Schnorr von Carolsfeld. (1) A man kneeling with his hands together, two great lions lying calmly on either side of him with their heads on their paws. (2) Five men standing in a row on a low hill at night, each holding a small clay lamp high. (3) A small young shepherd with a sling facing a giant twice his height. (4) A shepherd standing, a lamb lying across his shoulders, four sheep at his feet. (5) A man and two girls hurrying to the right, a figure ahead leading the man by the hand; a woman behind them standing still and looking back. (6) A man kneeling alone, very small, under one tall shaft of light falling from above; nothing in the light. Plain, timeless clothes: robes and cloaks, no armour, no ornaments. Restrained, reverent, gentle: for children aged 7 to 10.

Give it one of the game's figure frames (Christian's book-free standing picture) as the style reference. Check every figure for drawn faces and for the far foot set higher (`tools/flat-feet.py`).

- Julius Schnorr von Carolsfeld, *Die Bibel in Bildern* (1852 to 1860), woodcuts on Commons: simpler, flatter compositions, the closer match for silhouettes.
